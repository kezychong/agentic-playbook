# Brand Build

Use this skill for a new brand or a foundation with important gaps. If most of the foundation exists and the task is strategic change, use `ap-brand-strategist`. If the task is verifying claims already made in public, use `ap-brand-audit`.

## Core rule

Do the research and drafting work, but do not make the user's decisions. Bring evidence-backed candidates instead of blank questions. A gap is safer than a polished generic answer.

## Working objects

Keep three objects separate in the user's chosen workspace:
- **Workbench:** current state, open questions, candidate decisions and next step.
- **Evidence records:** dated sources, observations, stakeholder statements and limitations.
- **Foundation:** only the decisions the responsible user has explicitly approved.

Suggested names are `strategy-workbench.md`, `evidence/` and `foundation/`, but follow the user's workspace conventions. Never assume a particular home directory or vault layout.

## Foundation areas

Build only the areas that are missing:
1. **Audience situation:** who the brand serves, in what circumstance, and what they are trying to change.
2. **Position:** the relevant alternative and the choice that makes this brand meaningfully different.
3. **Promise:** the outcome or value the brand can credibly commit to.
4. **Proof:** operational facts, evidence and limitations that make the promise believable.
5. **Personality:** behavioural traits, voice boundaries and choices the brand consistently makes.
6. **Relationship:** the role the brand plays before, during and after purchase.
7. **Recognisable expression:** visual, verbal and sensory principles that a qualified designer or writer can apply.

These are generic working areas, not a proprietary named method.

## Procedure

### 1. Establish scope

Confirm the brand, decision-maker, business model, audience, maturity, deadline, workspace and permitted information. Inspect existing foundation material before asking questions. Do not overwrite settled decisions unless the user explicitly reopens them.

### 2. Create the workbench

For each area, mark `missing`, `in progress`, `approved` or `reopened`. Record known evidence, gaps, dependencies and the next decision. Status must match the actual content; never label a section approved because a draft exists.

### 3. Gather irreplaceable input

Ask for facts only the user can supply: origin, convictions, real customer language, delivery constraints, operating choices and measured results. For everything else, research or draft candidates first. Label stakeholder statements separately from independently checked facts.

### 4. Draft candidates

Work one area at a time. Present two or three candidates that differ in substance. Each candidate includes:
- exact proposed wording;
- evidence and source date;
- assumptions and missing proof;
- implications and trade-offs;
- what must be true for the choice to hold.

Never invent customer quotes, performance figures, awards, product capabilities, design assets, typeface rights or colour values.

### 5. Record the decision

Ask the responsible user to choose, edit, defer or reopen. Silence and general encouragement are not approval. When exact wording is approved, record that wording and the decision date in the foundation, then update the workbench. Keep rejected candidates in the workbench only if they remain useful context.

### 6. Check coherence

After all seven areas are approved, check:
- audience, position and promise describe the same buying situation;
- proof supports the promise at its stated scope;
- personality and relationship reinforce rather than contradict the position;
- expression principles are usable without fabricating assets;
- operational behaviour can deliver the brand claim;
- the foundation remains distinctive after removing the brand name.

Reopen conflicts rather than smoothing them over with vague language.

### 7. Close the build

Deliver the foundation, evidence index, unresolved risks and a short handoff for strategy or content planning. The foundation is an internal source of truth, not permission to launch or publish.

## Output format

```markdown
# Brand foundation: [brand]
Status: draft | partially approved | approved internally
Decision owner: [role]

## Audience situation
[decision, evidence, limitations]

## Position
[decision, alternatives deliberately not chosen]

## Promise
[decision and scope]

## Proof
[dated evidence and gaps]

## Personality
[traits, behaviours, always / never]

## Relationship
[role and journey expectations]

## Recognisable expression
[principles and designer/writer brief]

## Open risks
[unknowns, conflicts, validation work]
```

## Verification

Before closing, confirm that each approved area has exact wording, a decision date and a responsible decision-maker; every proof claim is traceable; assumptions remain visible; no private or unpermitted material crossed the agreed boundary; files were saved only where authorised; and nothing was published.
