# Brand Strategist

Use this skill for positioning, audience choices, competitive diagnosis, brand-led go-to-market planning, or a brand that is underperforming. Use `ap-brand-build` when the foundation is missing and `ap-brand-audit` when the main question is whether existing claims are supportable.

## Inputs

Collect only what is needed:
- brand, offer and business model;
- objective, deadline and decision to be made;
- intended audience and buying situation;
- known constraints, prior decisions and available evidence;
- permitted research sources and confidentiality boundary.

If a missing input would change the recommendation, ask. Otherwise proceed with a labelled assumption.

## Evidence discipline

Maintain an evidence map with four labels:
- **Observed:** present in a supplied record or retrieved source.
- **Stakeholder statement:** stated by a responsible person but not independently verified.
- **Inference:** reasoned from observations; show the reasoning.
- **Unknown:** important but not established.

Record a source and date for every material fact. Never invent customer quotes, results, market size, awards, competitor behaviour or product capability. Strategy documents are context, not proof that customers think or act as described.

## Procedure

### 1. Frame the decision

Write one sentence: “We need to decide [decision] for [audience/situation] to achieve [objective] within [constraints].” List out-of-scope questions. Confirm the selected brand and permitted information boundary before research.

### 2. Build the evidence map

Organise evidence under customer situation, alternatives, category conventions, brand capabilities, business model and delivery constraints. Separate facts from beliefs and unknowns. Use live research tools for outward-facing facts; if retrieval fails, mark the gap rather than filling it from memory.

### 3. Diagnose the strategic problem

Answer:
1. What job is the audience trying to complete, and in what situation?
2. What do they use or do instead?
3. Which category claims are crowded or interchangeable?
4. Which capabilities and operating choices can this brand prove?
5. Where does the current message, offer or experience break the path to the objective?

Name the root problem and the evidence that supports it. Do not prescribe tactics before this diagnosis exists.

### 4. Develop three real options

Create up to three options that differ in strategic choice, not wording. Each option must state:
- priority audience and situation;
- problem or tension addressed;
- value offered and credible reason to believe;
- what the brand will emphasise and deliberately not emphasise;
- business, product, pricing, experience and communication implications;
- evidence strength, major assumption and disconfirming test.

Reject any option that could describe most competitors after removing the brand name.

### 5. Compare and decide

Score options qualitatively against relevance, distinctiveness, credibility, business fit, operational feasibility and evidence risk. Show trade-offs; do not hide them in a total score. Recommend one option, but leave the decision to the responsible user. Record the chosen option, rejected alternatives, reasons and unresolved assumptions.

### 6. Turn the choice into an activation plan

For the chosen option, draft:
- one positioning paragraph;
- message hierarchy: core value, supporting points and proof;
- experience or operating changes required for the promise to be true;
- channel roles based on the audience's decision journey;
- a short test sequence, with owner, input, output and stop condition.

Do not write production copy unless asked. Do not treat a strategy decision as permission to publish.

### 7. Measure and learn

Choose measures that match the objective and available data. Record baseline, observation window and confounders. Distinguish leading signals from business outcomes. A movement in metrics is evidence to investigate, not automatic proof of causation.

## Output format

```markdown
# Brand strategy draft: [brand]
Status: draft
Decision: [one sentence]

## Evidence map
[observed / stakeholder statement / inference / unknown, each sourced]

## Diagnosis
[root problem, affected audience, evidence and gaps]

## Options
[three options with trade-offs and tests]

## Recommendation
[choice, why, risks and required decision]

## Activation plan
[message hierarchy, operating implications, channels and tests]

## Measurement
[baseline, measures, window and review date]
```

## Verification

Before calling the draft ready for decision, confirm:
- every material factual claim has a source and date;
- beliefs and inferences are not presented as facts;
- options are strategically different;
- the recommendation fits delivery and business constraints;
- proof supports the proposed promise;
- unknowns, privacy limits and research failures are visible;
- no external action or publication has occurred.
