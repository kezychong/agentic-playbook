
# M1. Capture

This stage catches raw ideas before they evaporate. You say the thing, in whatever mess it comes out, and your agent files it as an Idea Card with everything around it filled in. Nothing gets polished, scored, or decided here. Capture is filing, not choosing.

There are two ways in: one idea at a time, or harvest mode, where the agent mines a whole call transcript or newsletter you already wrote and pulls out the idea candidates hiding inside it.

## YOUR JOB vs MY JOB

**Your job**

- Say the raw thing. Rambling is fine. Half-formed is fine. Voice notes are perfect.
- Write THE ANGLE, when you are ready. Your one-line take on this idea. Nobody else writes it, ever (law 5, angle-is-yours). You do not need to touch the file yourself: say the angle out loud and I will put it on the card word for word, as dictation. Dictation still counts as you writing it. What I can never do is invent it, suggest it, or polish it.
- Answer the three short questions the agent asks after filing, or say "pass" and be done.

**My job (the agent)**

- Save your words exactly as you said them, character for character. No cleanup, no tidying, no summarizing.
- Fill in everything around the angle: where it came from, when, what channel, and any of the six questions your own words already answered.
- Leave THE ANGLE blank and say so out loud when I report back.
- Never invent, guess, or "best effort" an angle, a hook, or a claim you did not make.

## Procedure: one idea (the normal path)

**Trigger:** "capture this idea", "content idea: ...", or just a voice note dropped in.

1. **Take the raw input exactly as given.** It can be a voice-note transcript, a link, a screenshot description, a comment someone left you, a forwarded message, a half-written conviction, a photo of something on your desk. No polished writing required.

2. **Open the Idea Card template** in `templates.md` and start a new file.

3. **Fill VERBATIM CAPTURE with the raw input, word for word.** This section is sacred. No grammar fixes, no paraphrasing, no shortening. If the source was a link or a file, paste the raw text and note where it came from underneath.

4. **Fill the source details.** Where it came from (voice note, message, comment, link, note), who captured it, the date and time, the raw source, and the watchlist account it relates to if that is obvious from the input. Leave a field blank rather than guessing at it.

5. **Attempt the six-question voice note**, but only where the raw words already answer a question. Never invent an answer into existence. A blank question is a valid, honest result:
   1. What happened?
   2. What surprised or bothered me?
   3. What do most people appear to believe?
   4. What do I believe instead?
   5. What evidence or experience supports that?
   6. Who most needs to hear it?

6. **Leave THE ANGLE and the proof line blank.** Exactly as the template ships them. This is a hard gate, not a preference. See law 5 in `laws.md`.

7. **Name the file** `YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM-short-slug.md`, using the time you captured it. The slug is lowercase, hyphenated, plain words, no abbreviations.

8. **Save it into `content-os/ideas/`.** One file per idea, always. Two ideas in one voice note become two files. If the input clearly bundles several distinct ideas, ask which way to split rather than deciding alone.

9. **Report back:** the file name, and one plain sentence stating that THE ANGLE was left blank and is waiting on you.

10. **PIE echo (live captures only).** Right after filing, the agent asks three short questions once, and records your answers word for word:
    - **Problem.** What pain does this touch?
    - **Importance.** Why does it matter? The agent may follow your answer with "and why does THAT matter?" up to two more times, to push past the safe version into the real one.
    - **Experience.** Where have you lived this? What actually happened?

    Rules on the echo: one pass only, never nagged, blanks are fine. If you say "pass", that is final for the session and the agent drops the thread immediately. It never re-asks later in a new wrapper. And none of your PIE answers may be turned into THE ANGLE. They are context sitting around the angle, nothing more.

    Skip the echo entirely in harvest mode and on drops that are not a live conversation.

## Procedure: harvest mode

**Trigger:** "harvest ideas from this transcript", "mine my call for content ideas", "pull content ideas out of this newsletter".

Same job, batch size. One transcript in, several candidate Idea Cards out. Everything above still applies, unchanged. Harvest mode only adds how the candidates get found and capped.

**What you can harvest:** your own material only. A recording of your own call, workshop, coaching session or webinar, or a newsletter you wrote. Never someone else's content. Mining other people's posts is a different stage with different laws, and it lives in `02-research-drop.md`.

1. **Read the whole transcript first.** No skimming, no sampling the first third.

2. **Find the candidate moments.** A candidate is a moment where YOU are speaking with conviction, teaching a mechanism, telling a story that carries a lesson, taking a position against the obvious one, or answering a question with a strong opinion. Other people's words are never candidates. Neither is small talk, scheduling, or the bit where everyone waits for the last person to join.

3. **File one Idea Card per candidate**, following steps 2 to 9 above.
   - VERBATIM CAPTURE is your actual words, quoted exactly, with enough sentences around the quote that it still makes sense on its own. Note the timestamp if the transcript carries one.
   - Source details: name the call or issue it came from, and where the transcript file sits.
   - Six questions: only what the quote itself directly answers.

4. **Cap it at about 5 to 8 cards per transcript.** Quality beats quantity. A real conviction, mechanism or story earns a card. A topic that got mentioned once does not. Three strong candidates is a better harvest than eight weak ones.

5. **Report the batch:** each file name plus one descriptive line saying what the quote is about. Descriptive only. A description is "the bit about why she stopped taking rush jobs". An angle is a position. The agent writes the first and never the second.

**The angle law bites hardest here.** Summarizing a transcript naturally wants to compress "the point" of what you said into one clean sentence. That sentence is an angle. Writing it, even from your own words, is still the agent taking your job. It stays blank on every harvested card.

---

## What this stage never does

- Never writes THE ANGLE, a hook, or a proof line.
- Never scores, ranks, or kills an idea. That is `03-qualify.md`, and even there the decision stays yours.
- Never cleans up, shortens, or "improves" your raw words.
- Never harvests content that is not yours.
