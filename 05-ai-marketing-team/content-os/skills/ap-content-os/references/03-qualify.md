
# M3. Qualify

This stage puts a number and a reason next to each idea so you can pick fast instead of staring at a pile. Seven questions, scored 0 to 2 each, plus a seven-point kill-list check.

The score is a pre-score. It is the agent showing its work so you can decide in thirty seconds. The choice of what gets made is yours and only yours. That is gate one: is this what I believe?

## YOUR JOB vs MY JOB

**Your job**

- Decide. Pick what gets made, park what does not, kill what should die.
- Overrule the number whenever your gut disagrees with it. You know your audience. The scorecard is a sorting aid, not a judge.
- Say why, in one line, when you overrule it. That line becomes a rule in `MY-RULES.md` and the scoring gets sharper next time (law 10).

**My job (the agent)**

- Score every idea against the same seven questions, with a real reason attached to every number.
- Run the kill-list and flag what it catches, plainly, without softening it.
- Write the result onto the bottom of the Idea Card and stop. I never move the file, mark it approved, or tell you what to do.

---

## Procedure

1. **Load the Idea Card.** The whole card: the raw capture, the source details, THE ANGLE, the proof line, the six questions, the PIE answers. An Idea Card with no angle written on it stops here. Score nothing. Say plainly that the angle is still blank and it is waiting on the member (law 5).

2. **Score the seven dimensions, 0 to 2 each.** Every score gets one or two sentences of reasoning that names the specific evidence on the card, or names its absence. Never a bare number.

   - **Conviction.** Do I genuinely care about this?
   - **Audience stakes.** Does this materially affect the intended viewer?
   - **Evidence.** Do I have experience, proof or strong reasoning?
   - **Distinctiveness.** Am I adding something beyond the obvious?
   - **Timeliness.** Is there a reason to discuss it now?
   - **Strategic fit.** Does it reinforce what I want to be known for?
   - **Visual potential.** Can I show rather than merely assert?

   Maximum score: 14.

   When the card does not carry enough information to score a dimension, say so out loud and score low. Never guess upward to be encouraging. A physiotherapist's card that says "people warm up wrong" with no story attached scores 0 on evidence, and saying that plainly is the useful part.

3. **Total it out of 14 and compare it to the working threshold of about 9 out of 14.**

   Say the threshold exactly as it is meant: a working threshold, moderate confidence, a way of prioritising, not a law. There is one named exception. Genuinely time-sensitive news can be worth making below 9 out of 14, if the facts are strong and your reading of them is genuinely useful. When that exception applies, flag it out loud. Never quietly override the number.

4. **Run the kill-list.** All seven, each answered yes or no with one line of reasoning. Kill the idea when:

   - It is interesting only because it is new.
   - I have no opinion beyond the headline.
   - I have not used the product but want to sound as though I have.
   - Any generic creator in my category could say exactly the same thing.
   - The hook requires exaggerating the evidence.
   - The video offers no proof, reasoning or lived example.
   - I cannot explain why my specific audience should care.

   A yes on any of these is a flag, not an automatic deletion. Surface it plainly. The member still decides.

5. **Append the result, never overwrite anything.** Add a new `## Qualification` section at the bottom of the Idea Card. Do not edit, delete, reorder or "tidy" a single existing section of the card.

6. **Stop.** Do not move the file. Do not change its status. Do not write "you should kill this" or "this one is the winner". Hand back the scored cards and let the member choose.

## Reading your own scorecard in thirty seconds

- **Low conviction, high everything else.** Park it. Content you do not believe is the exact content that sounds like everyone else's.
- **High conviction, low evidence.** Do not kill it. Go and get the evidence. An e-commerce skincare founder who believes small batches beat big runs but has no numbers yet has a great idea sitting one experiment away from being makeable.
- **High score across the board, and a kill-list flag on "any generic creator could say this".** Trust the flag over the total. That flag is the one that predicts a forgettable post.
- **Everything scores 10 or 11.** Your cards are too thin, not your ideas. Go back to `01-capture.md` and let the PIE questions dig.

---

## What this stage never does

- Never decides. Never files an idea as approved, parked or dead.
- Never invents a dimension, moves the threshold, or softens a kill-list condition.
- Never scores a card whose angle is blank.
- Never phrases a score as an instruction.
