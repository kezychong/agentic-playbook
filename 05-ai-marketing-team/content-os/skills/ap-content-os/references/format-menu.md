
# The format menu: twelve repeatable conversations

## What this file is

A format is a repeatable conversation with rules your audience learns to recognise. It gives them familiarity without you making the same video twice. These twelve cover almost everything a business owner can honestly make.

The member picks **three to five** of these as their starters and they get written into `content-os/MY-FORMATS.md`. Not all twelve. A person who tries to run twelve formats runs none of them.

**Agent instructions for the pick:** show the list in plain language, a few at a time, not as a wall. Recommend candidates based on what they told you in the business and story parts of the interview, and say why in one line each. If they pick more than five, ask which three they would make this month, and start with those.

---

## How to pick well

Aim for a spread, not a set of near-twins:

- One that is fast to make on a normal week.
- One that shows real proof from inside your work.
- One that says something you believe and other people in your field would argue with.

A career coach whose whole menu is reviews and comparisons will be useful and forgettable. A skincare founder whose whole menu is convictions will be loud and unproven. The mix is the point.

---

## The twelve

Each card gives the **job** it does, the **shape** it follows, and **when it wins**.

### 1. The First Reaction
- **Job:** capture real surprise at the moment of discovery.
- **Shape:** what I expected, what happened, the moment that surprised me, why it matters, what I want to test next.
- **When it wins:** when something genuinely just happened to you and the surprise is still on your face. Film it same day or do not film it.
- **Guardrail:** this must not become your whole channel. Surprise with no substance behind it burns out.

### 2. The Tested Review
- **Job:** get past first impressions of a tool, product, method, or supplier.
- **Shape:** the job I gave it, how I tested it, what it did well, where it failed, who should use it, my verdict.
- **When it wins:** when your audience is already deciding whether to buy the thing and nobody has tested it under real conditions.

### 3. The [Your Method] Test
- **Job:** connect anything new directly to the one promise your business makes.
- **Shape:** the outcome I am protecting, what I tried, how I set it up and supervised it, what actually improved, the new problem it created, verdict.
- **When it wins:** when you have a named standard of your own to judge things against. A bookkeeping firm owner runs "The Clean Books Test". A physiotherapist runs "The Pain-Free Squat Test". Name it after your promise, then everything new gets judged against it, and your standard becomes the thing people quote.

### 4. The Build Log
- **Job:** build something in public, week by week.
- **Shape:** what I am trying to fix, what I built or changed, what happened, what broke, what I changed next, current result.
- **When it wins:** when you are mid-project and the ending is genuinely unknown. Honest and unfinished beats polished and retrospective.
- **Guardrail:** a build log that is calm and well-proven often still sits flat. If the progress is real but the reaction is quiet, front-load it with the moment that surprised you, format 1 style.

### 5. The Failure Autopsy
- **Job:** earn credibility by showing where it goes wrong.
- **Shape:** the promise, what I expected, the failure, the hidden reason, what a human still had to do, the corrected version.
- **When it wins:** when your feed is full of people showing only wins. The market is thick with successful demos. Honest failures are rare and more useful.

### 6. The Head-to-Head
- **Job:** compare two options around one real job.
- **Shape:** one clear task, same inputs, same criteria, side by side, trade-offs, winner by use case.
- **When it wins:** when your audience is stuck between two named things. The winner should not always be the newest option.

### 7. The Hype Autopsy
- **Job:** interpret a claim everyone in your field is repeating.
- **Shape:** what everyone is saying, why it sounds convincing, what my testing showed, where the claim holds, where it becomes nonsense, my verdict.
- **When it wins:** when a piece of advice has gone from useful to slogan and you have tried it on real clients.

### 8. News to Consequence
- **Job:** move from information to implication.
- **Shape:** the headline, what most people are repeating, what they are missing, who is affected, what changes next, my read.
- **When it wins:** when something happened in your industry this week and everyone is reporting it while nobody is saying what it does to the person watching.

### 9. The Conviction Memo
- **Job:** state a clear belief that some people will argue with.
- **Shape:** the common belief, my opposing position, why I believe it, lived evidence, the strongest objection to me, my boundary, conclusion.
- **When it wins:** when you have held the belief long enough to have paid for it. The objection beat is not optional. Without it a controversial opinion becomes an applause machine instead of thinking.

### 10. The Story or Scar
- **Job:** build trust through something that actually happened to you.
- **Shape:** scene, tension, decision, consequence, lesson, application.
- **When it wins:** when the lesson is one nobody would believe as advice but everybody believes as a story.

### 11. The Comment Courtroom
- **Job:** continue a conversation that is already happening.
- **Shape:** show the exact comment, name the claim inside it, agree with what is true, challenge what is wrong, give evidence, conclude.
- **When it wins:** whenever a post of yours got real disagreement. One busy comment section can supply weeks of follow-ups.

### 12. The Practical Teardown
- **Job:** teach through a real asset.
- **Shape:** show the thing (the checklist, the workflow, the plan, the output), explain what each part does, name the bottleneck, improve it, show the result.
- **When it wins:** when you want the piece to convert, not just to reach. This is the format that turns abstract advice into visible proof, and it is usually the one that earns enquiries.

## The three lanes: how fast a piece has to be made

Different ideas need different production speeds. Every format runs down one of these lanes.

**Lane 1, same-day interpretation.** For news, releases, price changes, regulation, public arguments in your field. Minimum before you film: a primary source, the known facts, what is still unknown, your interpretation, who is affected, one useful consequence. Shape: headline, missing context, my read, why it matters, what I am watching next. Rule: this lane values speed, never speed at the cost of inventing facts.

**Lane 2, tested experiments.** For reviews, method tests, before and afters, comparisons, client case studies. Minimum: a real job, a defined test, actual outputs, observed strengths, observed failures, a verdict. Shape: problem, experiment, evidence, trade-off, verdict, business consequence. This is usually the strongest authority lane for a working business owner, because nobody can copy your results.

**Lane 3, evergreen conviction.** For beliefs, principles, personal stories, long-held positions about your craft or your life. Minimum: a real conviction, a lived example, an acknowledged objection, a practical application. Shape: common belief, my position, story or reasoning, objection, principle, application. This lane can be batched, because it does not go stale.

## Two depth dials

These are separate measurements and a piece can be high on one and low on the other.

### Dial one: how deep the claim goes

| Level | Name | The claim sounds like | What it earns you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Information | "This thing was just released." | Nothing. Useful and completely replaceable. |
| 2 | Interpretation | "Here is what it actually changes, and what people are misreading." | Judgment. |
| 3 | Evidence | "I used it in my real work. Here is what worked and what failed." | Credibility. |
| 4 | System | "Here is how I built it into how my business runs." | Repeatability. |
| 5 | Consequence | "Here is what this changes about work, money, or how people live." | Meaning. |

**The floor:** every news piece must reach level 2. Most of your core pieces should reach 3 or 4. Your strongest brand pieces connect the experiment to level 5. Level 1 on its own is commodity, and a wedding photographer reposting a camera launch with no read on it is competing with everyone who can read a press release.

### Dial two: how much of you is in the story

A separate dial, from an external story-depth framework, measuring how exposed you are rather than how defensible the claim is.

- **Safe.** Surface. True, but anyone in your field could have said it.
- **Real.** Your personal stake is in it. It cost you something.
- **Raw.** The wound or the origin underneath the stake.

The scripting stage reaches these with a short "why does that matter to you" drill, and it stops the moment you decline. Depth floors per format:

- **Raw floor:** 9 The Conviction Memo, 10 The Story or Scar.
- **Real floor:** 1 The First Reaction, 3 The [Your Method] Test, 4 The Build Log, 5 The Failure Autopsy, 8 News to Consequence, 11 The Comment Courtroom.
- **Safe is fine:** 2 The Tested Review, 6 The Head-to-Head, 7 The Hype Autopsy, 12 The Practical Teardown. These are utility formats and forcing personal exposure into them makes them worse.

A below-floor piece gets flagged, never silently blocked. The owner decides how much of themselves goes on camera, every time.
