# Content Plan

Use this skill to plan a batch of organic content from a business objective. Do not use it for paid-media allocation, one isolated caption, or brand strategy that has not yet been decided.

## Modes

- **Plan:** create a batch from objective to draft briefs.
- **Capture:** add real customer language, scenes or proof to the source register.
- **Log:** record observed performance without explaining it prematurely.
- **Review:** compare results with the batch hypothesis and propose the next test.

## Inputs

Confirm:
- brand and business objective;
- planning window and deadline;
- intended audience and desired next action;
- production capacity by format, not aspirational volume;
- active platforms and language;
- existing strategy, offers and constraints;
- permitted evidence sources and confidentiality boundary;
- available baseline and prior results.

Use strategy documents as context. Do not treat their customer quotes, personas or market assertions as evidence unless each item has traceable provenance.

## Evidence labels

Every source item receives one label:
- **Captured:** traceable customer language, observation or real scene with date and source.
- **Verified:** checkable fact supported by a cited record or primary source.
- **Stakeholder statement:** supplied belief or recollection, not independently verified.
- **Constructed:** workshop language, persona text or illustrative copy.
- **Unknown:** required evidence not yet available.

Constructed material may inspire a question or angle, but never becomes a quote, result or proof point in a draft.

## Plan mode

### 1. Frame the batch

Write one sentence: “During [window], this batch should help [audience] move from [current state] to [next action] in support of [business objective].” Record capacity, platforms, language, constraints and baseline. If the objective cannot reasonably be influenced by content, say so.

### 2. Build the source register

Inventory captured customer language, product facts, founder experience, demonstrations, objections, case evidence and current public facts. Record source, date, permission and evidence label. List missing material as capture requests. Never manufacture a scene to fill a plan.

### 3. Diagnose the communication job

Identify the main obstacle to the desired next action:
- the right audience does not notice the brand;
- they do not understand the problem, method or value;
- they do not believe the claim;
- they do not know what to do next;
- existing customers lack guidance or reinforcement.

Choose one primary obstacle and explain the evidence. Multiple obstacles may appear, but the batch needs one priority.

### 4. Propose message directions

Create two or three directions that differ in viewpoint, not phrasing. Each includes:
- audience situation;
- central idea and useful tension;
- evidence available;
- content jobs it can support;
- risk, missing proof and what would disconfirm it.

Reject directions that could belong unchanged to most competitors. Ask the responsible user to select or revise one direction before producing the batch brief.

### 5. Allocate real capacity

Calculate the number of pieces from actual weekly capacity and the planning window. Then allocate pieces across the communication jobs needed for the selected direction. Do not import a universal ratio. Explain each allocation and keep at least one unit for a required job only when the evidence and capacity support it. If capacity is too small, narrow the audience, platform or objective instead of pretending the plan is larger.

### 6. Create the batch brief

Produce a compact table for approval before drafting:

| ID | Communication job | Angle | Evidence or scene | Format | Platform | Intended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Each row must contain traceable evidence or `NEEDS CAPTURE`. A `NEEDS CAPTURE` row may remain in the plan, but it cannot move to draft. Ask the responsible user to approve or edit the table as a planning decision. This approval is not permission to publish.

### 7. Derive the content map

Turn approved rows into production briefs. For each piece state the audience moment, one promise, evidence, structure, format constraints, call to action, owner and status. One source may support several pieces only when each piece makes a meaningfully different argument. Avoid duplicate hooks wearing different words.

### 8. Draft when requested

Draft only rows with adequate evidence. Preserve the chosen language and the brand's observed voice; do not imitate a living creator or paste from swipe material. Mark claims needing verification. Keep working notes, source citations and private names outside paste-ready copy.

Run these checks:
- removing the brand name would not make the piece interchangeable;
- the opening promise is fulfilled by the body;
- every quote, number, result and capability is traceable;
- one piece has one communication job and one intended action;
- urgency and scarcity are real, dated and relevant;
- no confidential or unapproved fact appears;
- the piece remains a draft.

## Capture mode

Record the raw item without rewriting it. Add source, date, speaker or record, permission, context and related obstacle. Distinguish exact quotation from paraphrase. If provenance is missing, label it unknown and do not use it as quoted evidence.

## Log mode

Record the metric, platform, observation time, denominator and source exactly as observed. Keep different formats, audiences and paid/organic distribution separate. Do not overwrite earlier observations. Logging records what happened; it does not assign a cause.

## Review mode

After the agreed observation window:
1. Check coverage and data quality.
2. Compare like with like and show the baseline.
3. Identify patterns as hypotheses, not causal facts.
4. Separate content quality from distribution, offer, timing and measurement effects.
5. Propose one or two changes for the next batch and what evidence would support them.
6. Ask the responsible user to accept, reject or modify the learning before it becomes a standing rule.


## Verification

Before delivery, confirm that the count matches real capacity; every draftable row has traceable evidence; constructed language was not presented as customer evidence; platform facts were checked when relevant; metrics include denominators and dates; privacy and permissions are respected; and no account was connected, scheduled or published through this skill.
