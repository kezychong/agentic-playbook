# Footage Story Assembly

Use for multi-speaker montages, testimonials, event highlights, sizzle reels, excerpt collections and a shorter evidence-led story assembled from longer recordings.

## Inputs

Confirm the playback context, intended viewer, target duration and format, complete source set, transcript status, speaker identities, consent scope, claim ledger, asset rights, disclosure duties, confidential material boundary, output folder and named reviewers.

A strong clip with unclear consent is a hold, not an asset.

## Workflow

### 1. Inventory the whole source set

Create one row per file with stable ID, owner, date, duration, dimensions, audio, location, hash when practical, permission and transcript status. Count expected and received sources. Do not claim complete coverage when files are missing.

Work from copies. Preserve originals and prior approved drafts. A request to edit does not authorise deletion.

### 2. Transcribe and identify

Transcribe every in-scope source when an approved local tool exists. Otherwise obtain supplied transcripts or review manually. Verify names, numbers and important phrases against the recording. Quarantine unidentified speakers and clips whose words cannot be checked.

### 3. Apply consent and claim gates

For every candidate clip, record:

- speaker identity;
- exact words and source time;
- intended use and audience;
- consent status and restrictions;
- disclosure needs;
- claim type and source support;
- confidentiality and sensitivity;
- include, hold or exclude decision.

Keep the speaker's own meaning. Revenue, profit, savings, forecasts and combined results are different claims. Do not add a currency, time period, customer relationship or result type the speaker did not state and the evidence does not support.

A restriction spoken in the recording counts as a hold even if no form was found.

### 4. Build the story spine

Choose one point per clip. Arrange a clear progression such as problem, effort, change and result. Start with a strong truthful beat and end with a useful conclusion. Do not sort clips only by the size of a number. The order must make sense to a viewer who knows none of the speakers.

### 5. Create the cut list as data

Record stable clip ID, source start, source end, speaker label, exact caption, claim IDs, consent status, position and reason. Keep this list separate from editor commands so a revision changes the plan rather than relying on memory.

Cut at complete phrases. Keep enough context to avoid changing meaning. Listen around every join and preserve the original audio unless an approved cleanup is needed.

### 6. Add captions and evidence

Captions must match the final audio. Carry the exact result type and scope. Use readable speaker labels only when identity is confirmed. Do not place a number on screen merely because it is more persuasive than the words.

Any document, screenshot, stage image or supporting clip needs its own rights and privacy check. A background glimpse of private data is still a disclosure.

### 7. Assemble and verify

Create a local review draft with an approved editor when available. Then:

- verify expected duration and streams;
- inspect frames around every join;
- check for black frames, frozen frames and clipped captions;
- listen for abrupt audio changes and cut words;
- compare all visible names and numbers with the ledger;
- re-transcribe the final audio when practical and compare it with the planned story;
- watch the full final file.

If the editor or checker is unavailable, deliver the cut list and review plan without claiming a render.

### 8. Handoff

Deliver the local draft, source coverage count, cut list, claim ledger, consent ledger, disclosure notes, verification performed, verification not performed, excluded or held clips and exact reviewer decision. Keep private originals private. Sending a draft to a speaker, client, sponsor or public host is a separate action outside this skill.

## Revisions

Preserve the previous review draft. Change only the requested clips or labels where possible. Rerun every affected claim, consent, join and playback check. A small wording change can alter the claim and must be reviewed again.

## Hard rules

- No public use without documented consent for that use.
- No unidentified speaker in a final draft.
- No relabelling or strengthening of claims.
- No hidden use of private recordings or account data.
- No synthetic face or voice replacement.
- No publication, send, paid tool call or live change through this skill.
- No “verified” claim without evidence from the final draft and its source records.

## Honest completion

Done means the requested local artifact exists and the stated checks ran against that exact version. A source inventory, transcript or successful encode alone is not a reviewed final video.
