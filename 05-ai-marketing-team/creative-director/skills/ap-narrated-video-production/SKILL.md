# Narrated Video Production

Use for narration-led explainers, slide-led videos, recorded teaching, product walkthroughs and a replay assembled from audio, captions and slides. For motion-heavy visual design, pair with Motion Graphics Production. For multi-speaker footage, use Footage Story Assembly.

## Inputs

Require the intended viewer, one learning outcome, approved source material, factual evidence, format, target duration, presenter, narration method, slide or visual source, rights and consent, accessibility needs, local output folder and reviewers.

Use only permitted human-recorded or owner-supplied narration. Synthetic identity processing is outside this package.

## Workflow

### 1. Classify the video

Choose one route:

- **feature walkthrough:** show one task, result and proof;
- **concept explainer:** build a mental model using examples and diagrams;
- **slide-led replay:** align supplied audio and captions to an existing permitted deck;
- **recorded teaching session:** prepare the presenter, freeze approved materials during the take and log corrections for the next version.

The route controls the depth. Do not stretch a simple feature into a long structure.

### 2. Write the teaching contract

State what the viewer can understand or do by the end, what prior knowledge is assumed and what proof will show success. Keep internal production labels out of viewer-facing copy.

### 3. Draft and approve the narration

Build the narration from verified facts and permitted experience. Use short, spoken sentences. Introduce one idea at a time. Mark uncertain facts and pronunciation questions. Do not invent a personal story, result, date, client or failure to make the script feel human.

Review the script before creating the full visual package. Script approval is not permission to synthesize audio, render, upload or publish.

### 4. Build the static visual gate

Before animation, make a still plan for every scene or slide. Each row records narration phrase, visual purpose, source IDs, visible copy, asset, accessibility note and reviewer status.

Check every still for:

- one clear hierarchy;
- readable type and contrast;
- accurate screenshots and labels;
- no private data, credentials or personal paths;
- no false clickable controls in passive video;
- no claim without evidence;
- no visual implying a future or proposed feature already exists.

Obtain approval of the still plan before a full render.

### 5. Prepare narration and timing

Use a permitted human recording or supplied audio. Verify the speaker's consent and usage scope. Create a transcript and phrase-level timing map from the final audio when tools are available. If timing is manual, label it manual. Never claim exact alignment from guessed timestamps.

### 6. Produce the video

For a new build, create scenes from the approved still plan and timing map. For a slide-led replay, map caption phrases to slide changes, preserve the supplied audio and show each slide long enough to read. For a recorded session, do not change what the presenter sees mid-take; log corrections and repair the next take or review draft transparently.

Keep real demonstrations faithful. Do not substitute a different interface or staged action without labelling it.

### 7. Mechanical checks

When local tools exist, verify the final file has video and audio, expected duration, dimensions and readable frames. Extract a chronological sample and samples around every major reveal. Check the beginning and end for clipped words or missing audio.

### 8. Human review

A reviewer watches the final file from beginning to end and checks each scene against the approved script and source ledger. Record:

- spoken phrase;
- expected visual;
- actual visual;
- timing status;
- claim and source status;
- accessibility status;
- pass, fail or needs review.

Source code, stills and a successful file probe do not replace playback review.

### 9. Handoff

Deliver the approved script version, audio provenance, timing method, visual map, local video, mechanical check results, playback table, rights or consent holds and exact next decision. Keep the status review-ready. Replacing a live video, changing a public description, uploading or sending is outside this skill.

## Failure rules

- Missing source fact: mark the gap and remove the claim.
- Unapproved still plan: stop before full production.
- Missing narration permission: stop before using the audio.
- Unavailable renderer: deliver the complete script, still plan and timing brief.
- Failed playback row: fix and rerun the affected checks; do not report the old pass.
- Unchecked final file: say playback review is pending.
