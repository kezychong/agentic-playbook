# Short Video Production

Use for a vertical or square talking-camera clip, reaction, demonstration, evidence-led social draft or short edit. Begin with an approved Creative Direction canvas when the message, evidence or rights are not already settled.

## Modes

- **Talking camera:** one speaker explains one useful point.
- **Demonstration:** a real process or product view supports the spoken point.
- **Reaction:** a permitted public source is shown and answered with an original viewpoint.
- **Reference-led draft:** the user supplies a reference for pacing or structure; reproduce principles, not protected assets, wording or a creator's identity.

## Inputs

Confirm the selected mode, intended viewer, one promise, target duration, aspect ratio, source footage, transcript, evidence sources, rights and consent, brand rules, accessibility needs and owner reviewers. If there is no recording yet, prepare a speaking map rather than pretending a finished script was recorded.

## Workflow

### 1. Inspect the real source

Inventory each clip's duration, dimensions, orientation, audio and ownership. Create a transcript with timestamps when a permitted local tool exists; otherwise ask for a transcript or work manually. Label automated transcription as unverified until names, numbers and claims are checked against the audio.

Never replace what the speaker actually said with a nicer claim. A draft script and a recorded take are different sources.

### 2. Check the message before polishing

Write the spoken point in one sentence. The first seconds should make the viewer understand the subject and reason to continue. The ending must deliver the promised useful point before asking for an action.

If the opening is tool talk, scene-setting or vague throat-clearing, propose a tighter opening for a new recording. Do not silently manufacture new spoken words inside an existing take.

### 3. Build the clean speech cut

Create a cut list before editing. Remove unintended repeats, dead starts and irrelevant detours without changing meaning. Keep natural breath and enough context for every claim. Preserve the original. Save each cut with source start, source end, reason and expected new position.

Do not cut on a partial word. Listen around every join. If exact audio review is unavailable, disclose that limitation and leave the join for human review.

### 4. Map visual beats

For each spoken beat, choose one treatment:

- speaker on screen;
- real demonstration;
- cited screenshot or document detail;
- simple diagram or text emphasis;
- licensed or owner-supplied supporting media;
- clearly illustrative generated media.

Prefer direct evidence over decorative metaphors. Crop evidence so its relevant line or value is readable on a phone while preserving enough context to understand it. Never fabricate posts, headlines, engagement, interfaces or receipts.

### 5. Design captions and layout

Use high-contrast captions, correct names and meaningful line breaks. Keep captions and essential graphics away from platform controls and frame edges. Check at actual phone size, not only on a large monitor. Do not cover a face, a demonstrated control or the evidence itself.

### 6. Handle references and reactions

Record the public source URL, author, date and access date. Separate the source's claim from the draft's opinion. Quote only what is needed and permitted. Link or cite the original where the format allows. A style reference permits analysis of pacing, framing and structure; it does not grant rights to copy music, footage, graphics, exact wording or a recognisable creator likeness.

### 7. Finish a review draft

Export a local review file only after checking the planned dimensions, duration, audio, captions and source map. Preserve an editable cut list or project. Label generated or illustrative scenes in working evidence so reviewers do not mistake them for real events.

### 8. Review gates

Before delivery, verify:

- the opening is clear without a caption or private explanation;
- the body fulfils the opening promise;
- the cut does not alter the speaker's meaning;
- every number, quote and result maps to the claim ledger;
- every depicted person and third-party asset has recorded permission for the intended use;
- demonstrations are real and do not expose private data or credentials;
- captions, names and subtitles match the final audio;
- evidence is readable on a phone and retains context;
- generated media is not presented as documentary proof;
- the final file has the intended video and audio streams when a checking tool is available;
- the reviewer watches the full final file, including every join;
- no paid tool call, post, send or live change occurred.

## Handoff

Deliver the review-ready file location, duration, dimensions, cut list, evidence map, consent or rights holds, checks actually run, checks not run and the exact reviewer decision needed. Do not call the draft published, approved or tested beyond the evidence available.

## After results

When the owner later supplies metrics, record the date, platform, audience basis and denominator. Treat patterns as hypotheses. Suggest one message, opening or visual test for the next draft. Do not fetch private metrics or connect an account through this skill.
