# Scoring, Red Flags & Rewrite Patterns (the full detail)

> SKILL.md gives the short version. This file is the full rubric, the complete red-flag list, and every rewrite move. Read it before you score and rewrite.

---

## The 11-dimension rubric

Score each dimension **1 to 5** (1 = the failure described, 5 = the strength described). The weights below sum to 100, so do not multiply the raw 1-to-5 score by the weight directly (that would max out at 500). Instead, each dimension's contribution is:

> **contribution = (score ÷ 5) × weight**

Add the 11 contributions for a total out of 100. A perfect 5 on every dimension sums to exactly 100. (Shortcut: sum every score×weight, then divide by 5.) Round the final total to a whole number.

**The mid-scale (3).** A 3 means the strength is *partially* present but generic or borderline: a real but un-branded mechanism, an implied but unnamed wound, one promise that is single-minded but flat. Use 4 to 5 when it is clearly, specifically done; 1 to 2 when it is absent or actively wrong.

**Two dimensions are gating.** If **Awareness-match** or **Sophistication-match** scores 1 or 2, the headline cannot exceed about **60/100** no matter how polished, because a headline talking to people who are not in the room, or one full stage behind the market, cannot be saved by clever words. The cap is a **ceiling, not a penalty**: if the computed total is already below 60, the lower number stands. Caps never raise a score.

| # | Dimension (weight) | A 1 looks like | A 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Awareness-match** (20) | Pitches product or price to cold traffic, or runs a coy unaware story at hot buyers; repeats the ad's cold hook on the page | Leads exactly where the clicked-through visitor stands; one stage ahead of the ad; continues the ad's promise |
| 2 | **Sophistication-match** (18) | Bare "get more leads / lose weight" claim in a Stage 3-4 market; no mechanism; competes on price | Leads with a named mechanism (Stage 3-4) or identity (Stage 5); the claim form fits how saturated the market is |
| 3 | **Specificity** (12) | Vague qualifiers: "many", "very effective", "best", "big savings", round numbers | Concrete, testable numbers, %, timeframes, named sources ("347 customers", "in 30 days", "40% lower cost"); odd numbers |
| 4 | **Single clear promise / curiosity** (10) | Stacks 4+ appeals; muddy; everything to everyone | One desire in one statement, OR one genuine open loop tied to a wanted result |
| 5 | **Rides an existing primal desire** (10) | Tries to manufacture a new want, or rides a low-urgency want (efficiency, neatness) | Channels one already-burning desire, high on urgency / staying-power / scope, rooted in one of the eight primal drives |
| 6 | **Belief carried, not just asserted** (8) | Big bold claim with zero credibility; triggers disbelief | Embeds a mechanism, number, authority, "even if", or "why you failed before" so the claim feels earned |
| 7 | **Right-prospect targeting / "you"** (8) | Clever but attracts the wrong reader; "we / our / I"; company-centred | Flags the exact buyer (wrong people self-screen-out); second-person; clearly answers "what's in it for me" |
| 8 | **Payoff, not feature** (5) | Leads with specs or what it IS ("made with titanium", "AI-powered") | Leads with the emotional or identity payoff, or what it DOES for the reader |
| 9 | **Wound resonance ("that's me")** (4) | Generic "Are you struggling with X?" surface restatement | Names the wound or pathway so it reads like prophecy ("You started fresh every Monday...") |
| 10 | **Opt-in container fit** (3) | Prices or names the product on a lead-capture page; tries to close the sale | Sells the lead magnet or event; the promise equals what the magnet delivers; one CTA |
| 11 | **Readability & human voice** (2) | Jargon, passive, abstract; fails the read-aloud test | Clear to a 12-year-old; active verbs; sounds like a real person; paints a mental picture |

**Banding:**
- **85 to 100** - ship and test.
- **70 to 84** - strong; fix the one or two flagged dimensions.
- **55 to 69** - a real weakness (usually a gating mismatch); rewrite.
- **Below 55** - wrong message for this market or awareness level; start over from the diagnosis.

Dimensions 1-2 come from the diagnostic spine in `references/awareness-and-sophistication.md`; dimensions 5, 8, 9 lean on the desire catalog and primal drives in `references/headline-principles.md`; dimension 10 comes from the program's own funnel method.

## The red-flag checklist

Run every headline against these. A gating flag (marked ★) caps the score.

- ★ **Awareness mismatch** - pitches product or price to cold/unaware traffic, OR runs a coy identity line at hot buyers. Talking to people who are not there is the number-one reason copy fails.
- ★ **Sophistication mismatch** - a bare claim ("get more leads", "lose weight") in a Stage 3-4 saturated market with no mechanism. Stage-1 copy in a Stage-4 world goes invisible and gets dragged into a price fight.
- ★ **Reuses the ad's cold hook on the page** - violates the Split Headline Rule; the visitor already moved up a level by clicking.
- **Prices or names the product on a lead-capture page** - wrong container; an opt-in trades an email, it does not sell.
- **Vagueness** - "many / very effective / best / quality / big savings / amazing". Every vague word is a credibility leak.
- **Big claim, zero belief scaffolding** - an outsized promise with no number, mechanism, source, guarantee, or "even if". Triggers disbelief, not desire.
- **Leads with a feature or spec** ("AI-powered", "made with titanium") instead of what it DOES for the reader.
- **Manufactures a new desire** instead of channeling an existing one, OR rides only a convenience want with no primal root. Gets clicks, not customers.
- **Goal dilution** - three or more stacked promises; tries to be everything to everyone.
- **Clever but wrong audience** - wit that attracts the wrong reader. A boring headline that attracts the right reader beats it every time.
- **Company-centred ("we / our / I")** instead of "you".
- **Past the believability ceiling** - a Stage-2 claim so inflated nobody believes it. Pivot to mechanism.
- **Empty curiosity / clickbait** - an open loop promising nothing the reader actually wants. Platforms punish it and the body cannot deliver.
- **Mis-calibrated fear** - pure dread with no doable next step, or so much fear the reader shuts down. Aim for medium fear plus a clear "you can do this".
- **Vague enemy** ("the system") where the market is sophisticated enough to need a specific, already-resented antagonist.
- **Surface pain only** ("Are you struggling with X?") with no wound resonance.
- **Fails the dual-readership test** - a scanner reading only the headline and sub-head would not get the core promise.
- **Echo-ad swipe** - a transparent copy of a famous headline that only works if product, market, and timing all happen to match.
- **Even or round list numbers** where odd numbers would read as more credible.
- **Fails the read-aloud test** - jargon, passive voice, abstraction. A confused mind says no.

## Rewrite patterns

Turn one weak headline into 3 to 5 stronger variants. **Spread the variants across more than one awareness level or sophistication stage** so the student can match them to their real traffic. Label each variant with its target (awareness L#, sophistication Stage #) and the formula used.

1. **Re-aim to the true awareness level.** If it mis-targets, rewrite to that level's lead: product + deal (L1), benefit + proof (L2), result (L3), problem (L4), indirect dream/fear/identity (L5). For an opt-in, step it up one level from the ad and strip the product if it is lead-capture.
2. **Inject a named mechanism** (the number-one saturated-market fix). If the market is Stage 3-4 and the headline is a bare claim, give the "how" a proprietary, ownable name drawn from the offer's real method, and lead with it.
3. **De-vague every word** (the specificity swap). "many" becomes a number, "effective" a %, "fast" a timeframe, "best" a comparison; round numbers become odd or precise. One verifiable figure beats any superlative.
4. **Crush the number-one objection with "even if".** Append the reader's biggest self-doubt and negate it. This single clause is often the highest-leverage opt-in lift, because it kills the objection right at the form.
5. **Climb the benefit ladder.** If it leads with a feature, ladder up: feature, then "so you can", then "which means", until you reach an emotion or identity payoff. Headline that.
6. **Name the wound, not just the pain.** Turn "Are you struggling with X?" into prophecy that names the pathway ("You started fresh every Monday. You told yourself this time would be different.").
7. **Add a single belief anchor.** Pick ONE: a number, a named source, a guarantee, a "why you failed before" frame, or a real enemy. Do not stack; one makes a big claim credible.
8. **Open or tighten the curiosity loop.** If it tells the whole story, withhold the mechanism or result behind "reveals / the secret / what X don't want you to know", but only when it is tied to a real wanted result. No empty clickbait.
9. **Cut to one promise.** If it stacks appeals, keep the highest-scoring desire (urgency x staying-power x scope) and delete the rest.
10. **Flip to identity for Stage 5.** If the market is claim-exhausted, drop the promise entirely and reflect the reader's role: "For the [identity] who...".
11. **Container-correct.** If it is a lead-capture page, remove price and product, make the promise equal exactly what the lead magnet delivers, and add the magnet type ("Free checklist reveals...").
12. **Humanise.** Read it aloud. If it does not sound like one person talking to one reader, swap jargon for plain words, passive for active verbs, and "we/our" for "you/your". Strip AI-writing tells: no em-dashes, no "unlock / supercharge / revolutionise", no "It's not X, it's Y" parallelism. Readers spot machine-written copy and it kills trust.
