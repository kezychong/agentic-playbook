# Story and evidence rules

Use this reference while outlining and fact-checking a deck.

## Seven story beats

1. **Opening promise** — the useful destination or central idea.
2. **Current situation** — the relevant state of the world or audience.
3. **Tension** — the cost, obstacle, misconception, or unresolved question.
4. **Reframe** — the idea that changes how the tension is understood.
5. **Path** — the method, choices, or sequence enabled by the reframe.
6. **Proof in use** — verified evidence, a documented example, or a clearly labeled scenario.
7. **Resolution** — the conclusion and next action, question, or decision.

A beat is a story function, not a fixed slide. Check the outline from top to bottom: each slide should advance one beat, and the transition to the next slide should be explainable in one sentence.

## Evidence ledger

For each factual slide, record this in the outline:

| Claim | Claim type | Source | As of | Deck treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exact statement | current, historical, supplied, derived, or scenario | title or link | date or “not time-sensitive” | state, qualify, label, or remove |

Keep source notes specific enough for another person to verify the claim.

## Current claims

A current claim can change with time: price, availability, product capability, policy, law, ranking, market size, office holder, performance figure, or “latest” status.

- Verify it close to delivery time with a reliable source, preferring the relevant primary source.
- Show an as-of date on the slide or in a clearly linked source note.
- Preserve the source's scope, geography, population, unit, denominator, and time window.
- Do not turn a source's estimate, forecast, correlation, or opinion into a fact.
- If sources conflict, show the disagreement or narrow the claim; do not silently choose the most convenient number.
- If browsing is unavailable or the user requests source-only work, label the claim “not independently verified” or remove it.
- A user-provided statement is evidence of what the user said, not independent proof that it is true.

## Numbers and derived claims

Copy exact values, signs, decimal precision, currencies, ranges, units, denominators, and qualifiers. Do not round, convert, or recompute unless the user asks. Label a derived value as derived and retain the formula and source values in the outline.

## Examples and scenarios

- **Documented example:** cite the source and keep its real constraints.
- **User-supplied example:** label it as supplied by the user when not independently verified.
- **Illustrative scenario:** mark it “illustrative” or “fictional”; never present it as a result or customer story.
- **Generated visual:** label it as a diagram or illustration when it could be mistaken for real evidence.

## Slide citations

Keep a short source label near the supported claim. Use a final sources slide or linked source list for full titles, publishers, dates, and URLs. A source list does not repair a slide whose claim cannot be matched to a source.

## Final evidence pass

- [ ] Every factual slide maps to the evidence ledger.
- [ ] Every current claim has a reliable source and as-of date, or is qualified/removed.
- [ ] Exact numbers retain scope, units, precision, and qualifiers.
- [ ] Derived numbers are labeled and reproducible.
- [ ] Scenarios, mockups, and generated visuals are labeled.
- [ ] Conflicts and unknowns remain visible.
- [ ] No quote, result, customer story, or source was invented.
