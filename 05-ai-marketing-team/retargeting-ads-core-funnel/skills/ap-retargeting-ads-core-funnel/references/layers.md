# The Three Retargeting Layers

Once the four audiences exist, you build three retargeting layers. Each layer is one campaign (or ad set) that targets one audience and excludes the next one down the funnel. That exclusion is what makes this precise: you only spend money on the people who reached a stage but didn't take the next step — the ones who fell through the cracks.

## The pattern

**Target audience N, exclude audience N+1.**

Excluding the next stage removes people who already moved forward, so you never waste budget re-selling someone who already booked, and you never show a "you haven't opted in yet" ad to someone who already opted in.

## The layer matrix

| Layer | Target | Exclude | These people... | Awareness | Ad angle |
|-------|--------|---------|-----------------|-----------|----------|
| 1 | Video viewers (30d) | Landing page visitors | Watched a video but never hit your opt-in page | Problem-aware, still cold | **Problem-based** |
| 2 | Landing page visitors | Booking page visitors | Saw your opt-in page but never reached the calendar | Solution-aware | **Solution-based** |
| 3 | Booking page visitors | Thank-you page visitors | Reached the calendar but never booked | Product-aware, not convinced | **Product / offer-based** |

## Why the message changes per layer (awareness mapping)

The higher someone is in the funnel, the less they know about you and your solution — so the lower their awareness, and the colder the message needs to be.

- **Layer 1 — Video viewers who didn't opt in.** They watched, but never even clicked to your page. They are the coldest of the warm — problem-aware at best, maybe just entertained. Your ad should **name the problem** and make them feel understood, so they finally click. Lead with the pain, not the pitch.

- **Layer 2 — Opt-in visitors who didn't book.** They saw your headline and landing page, so they understand the problem you solve. What they haven't done is believe your *approach* is the way out. Your ad should be **solution-based** — show that your method is the path, not just that the problem exists.

- **Layer 3 — Booking page visitors who didn't book.** They got all the way to the calendar. They know the problem and they know your solution. What's holding them back is the offer itself — they aren't convinced it's worth it, or something about the commitment stopped them. Your ad should be **product/offer-based** — speak to the offer, handle the hesitation, and push them over the line to book.

This is the whole game: slice the audience by how far they got, then meet each slice with exactly the message its awareness level needs.

## Setup notes

- Build each layer as its own campaign or ad set so you can read performance per layer.
- Set the **target** audience and the **exclude** audience in the ad-set audience settings.
- Layer 3 is the smallest, warmest, and usually cheapest per result — it's people already at your calendar. Don't judge it by raw volume; judge it by bookings.
- Keep the layers running together. Someone can move from layer 1 to layer 2 to layer 3 over time as they progress through your funnel — the exclusions automatically shift them to the right message.
