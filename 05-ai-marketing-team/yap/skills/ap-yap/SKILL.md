# Yap

A yap video is rough, honest talking on camera, filmed on your phone.
You speak in your own words, then trim the recording to under two minutes.

Adapted from the supplied teaching method; repackaged and maintained by Kezy.

## Who this is for

You are a business owner or realtor who uses the Claude app or the Codex app.
You do not need technical skills. Your agent does all setup and file work.
You answer questions, choose an idea, and record yourself.

## The idea in five lines

- Rough talking can work without fancy production.
- Talk about your work, your interests, and your life. One topic only is boring.
- Post once a day, using a weekly rhythm.
- Your first 100 videos are gym reps: practise and learn what people want.
- Show how you think; save funny and trend videos until you have followers.

## First run: build the Yap Profile

Read [the doctrine](references/yap-doctrine.md) before interviewing the member.
Ask one or two questions per message. Use [the profile template](assets/yap-profile-template.md).
Learn their niche, audience, interests, journey, real voice, and whether they already have followers.
Ask what the intended viewer has actually experienced and whether they already know the member.
Reflect their answers back. Save only what they confirm.
Create a folder named `My Yaps` in their working space. Keep their profile, maps, and tracker there.
Fill and maintain the templates yourself. Tell the member where you saved their work; never ask them to handle files.

Set the cadence: Monday, Wednesday, Friday for the niche; Tuesday, Thursday for interests; Saturday, Sunday for life.
The target is once a day. Use the member's local day when choosing today's lane.
Start their 100-video tracker from [the tracker template](assets/yap-tracker-template.md).
Offer a later market scan: ask for or help build a list of 30 to 50 creators in their market.
Read [the market scan guidance](references/idea-lanes-and-kill-list.md). This optional step never blocks a yap.

## Every run: the five steps

### Step 1. Pick the lane for today

Read the profile and cadence. Tell the member today's lane in plain words.
For later runs, ask for today's lane if their day or saved cadence is unclear.
If they supplied an idea, place it in a lane and keep it available for their choice.

### Step 2. Find the idea

Read [the idea lanes and kill list](references/idea-lanes-and-kill-list.md).
Use niche, interest, or life ideas that fit today's lane and the confirmed profile.
Run the kill list before proposing five ideas. The member picks one.
Frame unconfirmed experiences as questions, never as things that happened to the member.
For a week of yaps, follow the cadence and repeat this choice for each day.

### Step 3. Truth interview

Ask two to four questions about the chosen idea: what happened, what they think, what proves it, and who it affects.
Keep questions short. Reflect the story, feeling, evidence, and viewer implication back for confirmation.
Never invent a fact, story, number, result, or feeling to fill a gap.
The member's lived story is theirs; do not fact-check it.
Check outside facts against their source where possible. Mark outside claims "check this" so uncertainty stays visible.
If proof or a feeling is missing, ask. Do not finish the map by guessing.

### Step 4. Write the yap map

Read [the map and gates](references/yap-map-and-gates.md). Fill [the blank map](assets/yap-map-template.md).
Write the exact opening and close. Keep the middle as five short bullet beats with a reason to keep watching.
Use Hook, Tension, Insight, Evidence, and Path. Keep a single fight: the claim or tension this video explores.
Add the on-screen hook, confirmed emotion, claim, proof, meaning, and viewer implication.
Use the five-part skeleton to shape the edges. Put proof right after the opening, then develop it in the Evidence beat.
Suggest a still picture or green-screen image only when it helps explain the topic. Otherwise use the member's face.
Run all four taste gates before showing the map. Rewrite failures using only confirmed material.
Tell the member which gate you fixed and what changed. If none needed fixing, say all gates passed.
If a gate needs missing truth, return to the interview before showing a finished map.

### Step 5. Record raw, then trim

Tell the member: "Film on your phone. Give yourself three to five seconds of real silence before the first line. Let the thought land first."
Keep that moment honest. Never ask for fake emotion. Speak from the bullets; do not read word for word.
After recording, ask them to say: "Here is my recording transcript, trim it."
If they arrive with a transcript, work on that recording using the truth rules and taste gates above.
Mark exact words to cut and the order to keep. Cut repetition and detours while preserving meaning, breath, and pauses.
Keep edits basic. Add captions or subtitles and keep it under two minutes, about two minutes is the target.
A transcript alone cannot prove the running time. Ask for the recording length when needed.
Tell the member to post the same video on Instagram and TikTok.
Posting or running an ad requires their explicit instruction. A finished map does not grant permission to publish.
Log confirmed posting details and supplied results in the tracker. Leave missing results blank.
Tell the member to check the views 48 hours after posting. Record them under "Views at 48h".

## Weekly review

Read the tracker. Report which lanes and hook lines earned new followers, alongside their "Views at 48h".
Use follows to judge what worked. Do not call a high-view video a winner without follower evidence.
Include direct messages or leads where the member supplied them. Say plainly when results are missing.
Flag winners as possible ads for later review. Do not launch an ad.
Adjust the weekly cadence from the recorded learning, and save the revised cadence in the profile.
Keep the first 100 videos focused on practice and learning.

## Hard rules

- Never invent facts or feelings. Use only confirmed personal material; outside facts get a "check this" flag.
- Never fact-check the member's own lived story.
- Script the edges. Bullet the middle. Keep one fight per video.
- No funny or trend videos until the member has followers.
- Build the member's personal brand, using their own voice.
- Run every taste gate before handing over a map, including revisions.
- Never assume what a viewer did or knows. Ground that framing in the interview.
- Do all file work for the member and tell them where their work lives.
