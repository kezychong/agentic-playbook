# Intake — Mining the Raw Material

The intake is where the challenge is really decided. The name, the daily arc, the pricing, and the back-end upsell all get assembled from what you pull out here. Do this before designing anything.

**Working rule: extract before you ask.** Read everything the user already gave you (their offers, past launches, audience notes, any existing course) and pre-fill. Then ask only for the gaps — in a short conversation, in phases, never a wall of questions.

There is no single template for a challenge offer. These questions are what shape THIS particular offer — the name, the duration, the daily rhythm, and the price all come out of the answers, not a fixed formula.

## Two files you create first

Create these in the user's own project folder (make a folder for the challenge if there isn't one). If they already exist, read them instead of re-asking.

### `audience.md` — describes their ICP

- Who exactly is this for? Who is it explicitly NOT for?
- What is the problem, **in the audience's own words** (not marketing-speak)?
- How badly does it hurt? Is this a "big pain" market (health, money, relationships) or a "nice-to-have"? Big-pain markets sell and retain far more easily.
- Where do they hang out online? Which channels can the user actually reach them on?
- How big and how warm is that audience *today*? (This is load-bearing — see the reach honesty note below.)

### `offer.md` — describes their offer ladder

- What outcome does the user help people achieve?
- Their current offers, top to bottom, with prices. Which is DIY, which is done-with-you, which is done-for-you?
- What can they actually fulfil, and what's the capacity ceiling? (The whole reason for a challenge.)
- What is the natural **back-end** offer the challenge should upsell into? (Coaching, group program, or a membership.)

**Why these files matter:** they stop the user re-explaining their business every session, and every asset the skill produces later reads from them. As the course presenter teaches: "You should now have an audience.md and an offer.md, so you don't have to keep repeating."

## The four intake phases

### Phase 1 — The bottleneck (confirm a challenge is even the answer)

- What's actually capping growth right now? Listen for capacity signals: full calendar, "can't find a slot," timezone limits, 1:1 doesn't scale.
- Have they considered "just selling a course"? Surface the fear: a plain DIY product where people don't get results can rebound and damage the coaching offer behind it. The challenge exists to solve exactly that — DIY *with* accountability.
- If the bottleneck is NOT capacity (e.g. it's traffic, or a weak core offer), say so. A challenge won't fix a demand problem. It may not be the right build yet.

### Phase 2 — The outcome (this becomes the name)

- What is the single concrete result someone could plausibly move on in a short window? Push for specificity: "lower your blood sugar," "land your first client," "swim 1km without stopping" — not "get healthier" or "grow your business."
- Get the timeframe the outcome genuinely needs. That sets the duration.
- **How long should this run?** 30 days? 4 weeks? 6 weeks? Something else? There's no default — pick the length that matches the outcome and what the audience can realistically stick with. A bigger transformation or a slower-forming habit needs more days; a fast, narrow win can be shorter. (See `offer-design.md` for worked examples at different lengths.)
- Whose transformation can they point to? Collect real before/after stories and numbers — these become the proof on the registration page.

### Phase 3 — The accountability reality

- Where will the community live? (A group they can host and actually show up in.)
- Can the user commit to **one live video a day** for the duration? If not, what cadence *can* they sustain? (The daily rhythm is the mechanism; a challenge with no daily action is just a course.)
- **What does the person who buys this actually DO, every day?** Not what you deliver to them — what they do with it. One small, concrete action, repeatable daily, that moves them toward the outcome. If the user can't name it per day yet, that's what Move 2 designs.

### Phase 4 — The back end (know it before you launch)

- What exactly gets sold at the end, and can the user fulfil it for this audience? (Overseas / timezone-limited audiences may rule out live 1:1 — group or membership may fit better.)
- What does that back-end offer cost, and does the challenge price ladder sensibly up to it?

## Reach honesty (do not skip)

Before designing, get an honest read on audience size and warmth. A challenge is a **launch** — it monetizes existing reach; it does not manufacture it. If the user has a large warm audience, a launch can be explosive. If they have little reach, the honest first move is building an audience or buying traffic — the challenge amplifies what's there. Never let the user believe the challenge itself will fill the room.

## Minimum before moving on

- The concrete outcome (Phase 2).
- The current offer ladder + the intended back-end upsell (`offer.md`).
- Audience size, warmth, and reachable channels (`audience.md`).
- Honest fulfilment capacity and a sustainable daily cadence.

With those in hand, proceed to `offer-design.md`.
