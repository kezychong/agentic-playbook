# Offer Design — Naming, Duration, Price, Accountability

Four decisions turn a vague idea into a sellable challenge. Make them in order.

Legend: **[CORE METHOD]** = the supplied method (the spine). **[RESEARCHED]** = credited best practice that extends the spine. Where they conflict, the core method wins.

## 1. The name — concrete outcome + timeframe

**[CORE METHOD]** Name the challenge around a concrete outcome and a timeframe. Example: **"30-Day Challenge: Lower Your Blood Sugar."** The outcome must be:

- Something a real person actively wants (not "get healthier" — "lower your blood sugar").
- Plausibly movable within the window (you can show measurable progress in the time given).
- Stated in the audience's own words from `audience.md`.

Test: read the name to a stranger in the niche. If they instantly know what they'd get and by when, it's right. If it sounds like a program title, it's too abstract.

## 2. The duration

**[CORE METHOD]** Duration is a deliberate choice, not a default — ask, don't assume. It doesn't have to be 30 days; it could be a 4-week challenge or a 6-week challenge. Pick the length that fits the outcome you're promising and how much stamina the audience has. One video a day, community accountability, then the upsell — that rhythm holds at any length.

**[RESEARCHED]** Formats at different lengths trade off predictably — pick by how long the outcome genuinely needs:

- **5-day challenge** — the short-sprint model: a quick win early, the framework in the middle, an "offer day" near the end. Great for a fast, single win; fills and completes at high rates.
- **7–9 day challenge** — *CommuniPass's 9-day framework* splits into build days + a bridge day + an offer day. A middle path.
- **30 days** — a common format that runs four weeks with a mission every weekday. Deeper trust and a higher-ticket back end, but harder to complete and to sustain daily delivery. This is the most widely used length, but it's one option among several, not the required length.
- **6 weeks (42 days)** — challenges run successfully at this length too, proof the mechanics hold beyond the 30-day format.

The tradeoff, stated plainly: **shorter = higher completion and easier to fill; longer = deeper trust and a bigger back-end offer justified.** (Sources disagree on the "best" length — FunnelKit favors 3–7 days for a fast win; other practitioners run 30 days, and still others run 6 weeks. There is no single right answer; match the outcome and the audience's stamina.)

## 3. The price

**[CORE METHOD]** Price it **low relative to the coaching offer behind it.** It's the entry rung. Its job is action and ascension, not margin. A cheap challenge into a fulfillable upsell is the whole model.

**[RESEARCHED]** Within "low," paid beats free for the outcome that matters:

- **Paid challenges see ~40–60% completion vs ~5–15% for self-paced programs** — payment is skin in the game (*CommuniPass, Scale For Impact*). Free maximizes raw reach but tanks show-up and completion.
- Practitioner price bands: **$27–$47** attracts serious participants and covers delivery; **$67–$97** signals higher value and pre-qualifies people who can afford the back-end offer. Some coaches report *better* engagement at $97 than $47. Even a $7–$27 entry filters intent versus free (*FunnelKit, CommuniPass*).

Guidance: if the goal is maximum front-end volume from a warm audience, a low paid ticket ($27–$47) is a strong default. If the back-end offer is high-ticket, lean $67–$97 to pre-qualify. Free only if reach is the sole objective and the user accepts lower completion.

## 4. The accountability layer (the ingredient that makes it a challenge, not a program)

**[CORE METHOD]** A challenge is "a DIY offer packaged with accountability." Two required pieces:

- **A community** — where participants gather, post, and are seen. A hosted community platform or a private group both work.
- **A daily action** — one thing to show up and do each day. "I'm going to show up every day and do one thing." This is what drives action where a plain program fails.

Name both concretely before moving on: exactly where the community lives, and what the single daily action is.

**[RESEARCHED]** What makes accountability actually convert to completion:

- **The Daily Loop** (*Scale For Impact*): show up → learn one thing → do one thing → post proof → get feedback → repeat. The "post proof" step is the engine — public commitment drives finishing.
- **Community-platform leaderboards + daily check-in threads** are cited as the single strongest completion driver (*CommuniPass*).
- **Multi-channel delivery** (email + one of WhatsApp/Telegram/Discord) raises completion by ~20–25 points over single-channel.

## Output of this move

Before moving to `funnel-design.md`, you should have written into the user's `offer.md` (or a challenge brief beside it):

- Challenge name (outcome + timeframe).
- Duration + one line of reasoning.
- Price + whether free/paid and why.
- The community home + the shape of the daily action.
- The named back-end offer this ladders into (confirmed fulfillable).
