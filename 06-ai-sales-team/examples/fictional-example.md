# Fictional example — a four-week follow-up challenge

Agentic Playbook — by Kezy

Company A teaches independent shop owners how to organize follow-up. It can host one weekly
45-minute group session and moderate a private community for 20 participants. The supplied call
says the audience wants a consistent contact routine. No sales lift or customer testimonial is supplied.
Price, cohort start and refunds are undecided. No payment processor or publishing is authorized.

Trigger: “Use ap-challenge-offer-creation to turn this into a draft offer. Ask only for missing facts.”

Expected result: audience.md and offer.md, a proposed four-week delivery sequence, an accountability
plan, dated launch parameters marked awaiting dates, a registration-page brief and a continuity
proposal. Week 1 organizes permitted contacts; week 2 drafts messages; week 3 rehearses the routine;
week 4 reviews completion. Outcomes describe completed practices, not guaranteed revenue.

Expected review flags: price, dates and refund terms need the owner; no invented testimonial;
capacity must remain at or below 20; follow-up messages remain drafts. A sample CTA is “Review
whether the next cohort fits your needs,” with booking details awaiting confirmation.
This fictional output illustrates the method; it is not evidence of campaign performance.
