# VSL doctrine: a video that books a call (15 to 20 minutes, coaching or advisory offer)

Every rule below is traced to the teaching it came from (the direct-response VSL school, the webinar-belief school, the RMBC copy school, the classic awareness model and others). Techniques carry generic credit. Read it as a lookup, not an opinion: when your agent proposes a structure, it should be able to point at the line here that allows it.

The structure follows the compressed webinar-belief spine for twenty minutes (intro and promise, quick origin, three belief shifts, invitation to a call). Section 2 composes it with the direct-response VSL school’s "series of agreements" order and evaluative-proposition close and the RMBC copy school’s unique-mechanism rules; the fit verdicts explain why.

## 1. Format ruling: why a 15 to 20 min VSL for a book-a-call coaching offer and when this doctrine says NOT to

### Default ruling for this use case

- Use the 15 to 20 minute VSL only when video matches the audience’s consumption behavior, the coaching outcome can be made understandable without self-paced technical study, and the page’s sole conversion event is an invitation to a fit call. This doctrine treats format as a tool selected for audience and product, not a universal rule. [TSL vs VSL is audience- and product-specific]
- Prefer video when the audience is younger or already accustomed to watching the seller. The direct-response VSL school’s rule of thumb is that younger audiences generally prefer video; do not transpose that rule to every market. [TSL vs VSL is audience- and product-specific]
- Treat 15 to 20 minutes as a conditional compression target, not a sacred duration. Existing intent shortens the sales process; low-awareness, no-intent traffic requires more time or a webinar because intent must be built rather than harvested. [Length is set by existing intent, not preference]
- Use the VSL to move the viewer through **Insight → Interest → Intent → Action**. Establish possibility, close the possibility-to-attainability gap, create intent around the outcome, then ask for the call. [Insight → Interest → Intent → Action]
- Keep the CTA delayed until intent exists. Compress the argument so the intent moment arrives before attention collapses; never solve weak retention by dragging the booking ask ahead of belief. [Delayed button; squish the VSL to the CTA]
- Make the close an invitation to a two-way fit conversation, not a disguised checkout. State what the call is, what it is not, who should book, and who should not. [the close is an invitation to a call]

### Attention to intent equilibrium

- Budget every minute as a trade: more time can raise intent and price elasticity, while the same time destroys attention and raises the cost per surviving viewer. [the attention/intent equilibrium]
- Reject length added for prestige, completeness, or “long-form” convention. This doctrine names both failures: a five-minute VSL with too little intent and a very long VSL with high intent but too few survivors. [the attention/intent equilibrium]
- Find the current intent-producing moment; remove nonessential material before it; move that moment toward observed drop-off; place the CTA immediately after it. [Delayed button; squish the VSL to the CTA]
- Do not equate paid attention with available attention. Buying the click does not make watch time free. [the attention/intent equilibrium]

### Traffic-temperature conditions

| Arrival condition | Format ruling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Warm, problem-aware, already trusts the seller | Use a shorter 15 to 20 minute VSL; harvest and complete existing intent. | High intent shortens the process. [Length is set by existing intent, not preference] |
| Solution-aware, has seen many coaching promises | Use 15 to 20 minutes only if the VSL can supply a credible mechanism, proof, and fit evaluation without generic coaching claims. | An aware market needs the “how,” while saturation changes which claim can still land. [In aware and hyper-aware markets, show HOW it is possible (the direct-response VSL school); market sophistication - the classic awareness model’s five stages, as written] |
| Cold but problem-aware, with high status and proof in this market | A straight book-a-call VSL is permitted; retain proof and qualification. | The direct-response VSL school allows straight-to-call when status, proof, and problem awareness are present. [low-ticket buy in front of the call] |
| Cold, unaware, no existing intent | Do not force the 15 to 20 minute cap; use a longer VSL or webinar, or build awareness upstream. | A big unaware market needs the seller to build intent. [Length is set by existing intent, not preference] |
| Traffic arrives from short, interruption-heavy ads | Do not jump automatically to a long VSL; preserve continuity and shorten. | Short preceding behavior predicts short available attention; the direct-response VSL school names TikTok-to-long-form as a poor match. [Match VSL length to the ad environment] |
| Traffic arrives from a long ad, training, community, or other high-consumption environment | A 15 to 20 minute VSL is congruent if the preceding asset has already built awareness or intent. | Length should reflect the ad environment and preceding behavior. [Match VSL length to the ad environment] |

### When this doctrine says not to use this format

- Do **not** use a fixed 15 to 20 minute VSL when the problem is deep, technical, or research-heavy and the buyer needs to pause, scan, compare, or reread. Route to a sectional TSL or long-form page. [TSL vs VSL is audience- and product-specific]
- Do **not** use video merely because it is faster to build. The five-minute-compression teaching defaults toward long-form as price and complexity rise and explicitly says to test the vehicle rather than canonize a rule. [pick the vehicle by price and complexity]
- Do **not** send unknown cold traffic straight to a free call when the seller lacks status and proof in this market. Add a stronger qualifier, including a low-ticket purchase where appropriate, before the call. [low-ticket buy in front of the call]
- Do **not** assume “coaching” traffic will tolerate another application funnel. When the market is burned out and recognizes the call trap, the direct-response VSL school’s recorded alternative is a diagnostic quiz that leads naturally to the call. [Tension - application vs quiz vs T2 vs country gate]
- Do **not** use a VSL when its mechanism requires webinar-level teaching depth to become believable. Choose the webinar when the mechanism cannot be demonstrated concisely enough to preserve the attention to intent equilibrium. [Webinar vs VSL by mechanism depth]

### Condition-based ruling where sources differ

- Resolve the five-minute-compression teaching versus the direct-response VSL school by condition, not preference: the five-minute-compression teaching pushes higher-priced or more technical offers toward long-form; the direct-response VSL school permits video for audience fit and treats high-ticket book-a-call as a valid three-agreement use. For this coaching offer, use 15 to 20 minute video only when complexity is compressible and the audience will watch; otherwise use self-paced text or a webinar. [TSL vs VSL is audience- and product-specific (the direct-response VSL school); The 3-step sales page]
- Resolve compressed-VSL versus chunked-infomercial guidance by observed attention. Squish when average watch time dies before the intent/CTA moment; use discrete desire chunks when attention is cluttered and the message already has multiple desires worth repeating. Do not treat the unconfirmed “close every cycle” idea as doctrine. [five-minute chunks, one desire each]

## 2. Structure options: at least three skeletons with a fit verdict for THIS use case

### Option A: the direct-response VSL school’s six-beat spine governed by serial agreements

**Sequence:** Awareness → Attention → Education → Sale → Close → Action. Underneath it, require agreement to attention, then the problem, then the mechanism/solution, then the product or next step. [problem, then mechanism, then product]

- Build **Awareness** with contrast: make the present condition different from the assumed condition. [Six-beat sales-message spine]
- Hold **Attention** with an unanswered juxtaposition or question; do not explain the answer before the viewer wants it. [Six-beat sales-message spine]
- Use **Education** only to show why the mechanism can produce the desired outcome. Do not open with education. [Six-beat sales-message spine]
- Translate **Sale → Close → Action** for high-ticket coaching as fit proposition → call invitation → booking, not product checkout. [the close is an invitation to a call]
- Keep the problem, mechanism, and next-step agreements separable enough that a failure can be diagnosed. This doctrine explicitly maps the three-step structure onto a three-part VSL and says it works for high-ticket book-a-call, including cold traffic. [The 3-step sales page]

**Fit verdict:** **Strong governing spine, incomplete as a spoken beat sheet.** Use it to police order and prevent the script from pitching before agreement. Add a story/belief pattern to carry the middle and a call-specific invitation to carry the close. [the close is an invitation to a call]

### Option B: the webinar-belief spine compressed to a book-a-call mini-webinar

**Sequence:** Intro + promise → quick origin story → Secret 1, vehicle belief → Secret 2, internal belief → Secret 3, external belief → book-a-call invitation. [20-minute mini-webinar and Six-part order as taught]

- Make each secret break one false belief; do not teach the viewer to completion. [20-minute mini-webinar]
- Preserve the order **vehicle → internal → external**. Secret 1 establishes the new way; Secret 2 breaks “I cannot”; Secret 3 breaks what the viewer treats as outside their control. [The vehicle/internal/external triad]
- Keep the origin quick and relatable, then move into belief work. [20-minute mini-webinar and Origin story]
- End on a call, not a value-stack purchase pitch. This doctrine’s exact 20-minute variant exists for cold paid traffic where the next step is a one-to-one consultation. [20-minute mini-webinar]

**Fit verdict:** **Best base skeleton for this use case.** It is the only cited option specified as a 20-minute narrated video ending in a booked call, and it supplies a disciplined middle: one story plus three ordered belief shifts. [20-minute mini-webinar and Production spec for the 20-minute video]

### Option C: application-funnel proof-and-connection skeleton

**Sequence:** Hook → Validate → Connect → phone. In the webinar-belief school’s version, proof earns the application, the application precedes a vulnerable connection video, and the next step is the inbound call. [Hook → Validate → Connect → phone]

- Use **Hook** to select the right prospect.
- Use **Validate** to establish proof and fit.
- Use **Connect** to counter second-guessing with a personal, vulnerable connection rather than another argument.
- Send the prospect to the phone only after these jobs are done. [Tension - last page before the call]

**Fit verdict:** **Use when proof is already strong and second-guessing is the main stall.** Do not force it into one continuous VSL if the funnel needs the application and connection as separate steps; the source teaches it as a page sequence. Prefer the compressed webinar-belief spine when one video must do all belief work. [Hook → Validate → Connect → phone (the webinar-belief school); the webinar-belief sequence - 20-minute mini-webinar]

### Option D: evaluative-proposition close as the whole argument’s destination

**Sequence:** orientation/evaluation → reveal potential → establish authority to evaluate → ask the natural next question, “now that you know the potential, would you like to know how to get there?” → booked call. [evaluative proposition that books the call]

- Make the evaluation real; skipping it leaves the flip with nothing to follow from.
- Do not lead with a brute-force “book a call” command or a money interrogation.
- Position the call as the place to determine the route and fit, not as a pretense for a guaranteed outcome. [the close is an invitation to a call]

**Fit verdict:** **Best close, not a sufficient full-body skeleton.** Attach it to Option B after the three beliefs have made the evaluation and next step credible. [evaluative proposition that books the call]

### Recommended composite

Use **Option B as the visible 20-minute sequence**, **Option A as the agreement/order control**, and **Option D as the close**. This is a condition-based assembly of cited components, not a new teacher-attributed framework: the webinar-belief spine supplies the exact 20-minute book-a-call sequence; the direct-response VSL school supplies the agreement logic; the evaluative proposition makes the call the natural next step. [20-minute mini-webinar (the webinar-belief school framework; a coaching-session teaching); the long-form VSL concept - Copy is a series of agreements (the direct-response VSL school); the application-funnel concept - The flip over]

## 3. The first 60 seconds: hook rules (the video-hook concept + Openings). What must NOT appear early (mechanism words, price, product)

### The job

- Treat the opening as an attention asset with one job: earn the next second. Do not ask it to explain the program, prove every claim, or complete the sale. [The hook is an asset with one job]
- Make the first line work after the speaker’s name is deleted. Never open with “Hi, I’m…,” an episode welcome, a logo bumper, or credentials. [Never open with yourself]
- Make the verbal hook sound spoken, not written. Read it aloud; if it would not be said to a friend, rewrite it. [Spoken hook delivery]
- Use clarity before cleverness. A six- or seven-year-old should grasp the hook’s basic meaning; confusion is the cardinal failure. [Clarity beats cleverness]
- Open on a recognizable problem, contrast, darkest relevant moment, or thought the viewer already has. Do not bury the emotional climax in the story. [Gut punch first (story-led opens) (the RMBC copy school); Contrast and callout]
- Use **you** and concrete, age-appropriate details. Relevance must become relatability. [Associative-state opening formula]
- Open a loop, then close it later. Curiosity without payoff becomes frustration. [Open loops and curiosity gaps]

### Required sequence inside 0:00 to 1:00

| Time | Required job | Test |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:03 | **Hook:** interrupt with a clear, relevant problem contrast or high-stakes recognition. | Would the intended prospect stop, while the wrong prospect could self-select out? [Contrast and callout] |
| 0:03 to 0:07 | **Power hook:** intensify or sharpen the unanswered question. | Is the second hook as strong as the first while the watch decision is still live? [the short-video hook teaching’s four-slot viral structure] |
| 0:07 to 0:15 | **Congruence:** connect the interrupt to the exact problem and promised understanding. | Does the opening pay off the ad rather than create a weird but unrelated pop? [Congruence and front-loading] |
| 0:15 to 0:35 | **Associative state:** where they were → where they are → what they do → why it is not producing the outcome. | Does the viewer recognize their lived sequence, not a generic category label? [Associative-state opening formula] |
| 0:35 to 0:50 | **Real problem:** “the real problem is not X; it is Y,” followed by the consequence. | Is Y concrete enough to picture without naming the proprietary solution? [Real-problem structure] |
| 0:50 to 1:00 | **Promise and loop:** state what they will understand by staying, not what they can buy. | Does this create possibility without collapsing the attention gap? [20-minute mini-webinar (the webinar-belief school framework; a coaching-session teaching); the long-form VSL concept - Six-beat sales-message spine] |

### Opening formula choices

- Default to **associative state** when the audience needs recognition: where they were, where they are, what they do, and why it does not work. [Associative-state opening formula]
- Use **timeline language** in saturated coaching: name the why, establish the easier past, mark what changed, show why the familiar tactic now arrives too late. [Timeline language in the why-what-how ad opening]
- Use **real-problem structure** when a descriptive run would be hard to follow: not X → Y → therefore → now you can see → future consequence. [Real-problem structure]
- Use **chunking down** only when a large claim must be made imaginable. Break the image into smaller units and preempt disbelief at each step; do not use unverified arithmetic or a system frame without proof. [Chunking down a big claim]
- For any result-plus-timeframe opening, retain the honest-difficulty disclosure and substantiate the result claim. [Fill-in-the-blank promo opening (timeline language)]

### What must not appear in the first 60 seconds

- **No mechanism name or proprietary mechanism words.** First secure attention and problem agreement; name the mechanism later at the transformation stage, before the product. This is the house timeline ruling produced by combining the direct-response VSL school’s sequence, not a claim that every teacher bans every mechanism-led headline. [Name it, count it, walk it, state the miss]
- **No product or program name.** The viewer must agree to the problem and then the mechanism before the product has anything to attach to. [problem, then mechanism, then product and No price and no product on pages 1 and 2]
- **No price.** Early price makes an unaware viewer orient the whole process around cost and leave; high-ticket/no-pitch variants explicitly keep “nothing for sale” in ads and opening minutes. [TSL vs VSL is audience- and product-specific (the direct-response VSL school); the webinar-belief sequence - High-ticket / no-pitch variant]
- **No booking CTA.** A CTA without intent is useless. Earn problem recognition, mechanism belief, and outcome intent, then reveal the action. [Delayed button; squish the VSL to the CTA]
- **No résumé, credibility parade, or impressive origin.** Identification precedes authority; the origin slot is relatable, not impressive, and comes after the hook. [Relatable, not impressive]
- **No education dump and no answer collapse.** Contrast creates awareness; the unanswered question holds attention; education follows only after both. [Six-beat sales-message spine]

### Source tension and ruling

- The mechanism teaching records that a mechanism can be named early when the delivery is liked and that a highly sophisticated market may require a mechanism in the headline. The present 15 to 20 minute coaching VSL instead withholds proprietary mechanism words for the first minute because its selected skeleton requires problem agreement and a relatable open first. Override this only when a tested control proves the market already wants the named delivery or when the brief explicitly chooses a Stage-3 mechanism headline. [Low-effort framing, delivery exposure, and reveal sequencing (the RMBC copy school); the classic awareness model’s mechanization: the third demand and the three stages (the classic awareness model); the long-form VSL concept - Copy is a series of agreements]

## 4. Awareness + sophistication ruling: problem-aware vs solution-aware traffic; what a saturated “coaching” market forces

### Diagnose two different variables

- Diagnose **awareness** as what this prospect already knows about the problem, the solution, and the seller’s product. [Five awareness levels]
- Diagnose **sophistication** as what the market has already absorbed from repeated competing claims. [the direct-response VSL school’s five-stage claim cycle]
- Do not use the labels interchangeably. A prospect may know coaching exists while remaining unaware of why prior coaching did not work for them; this doctrine calls these transitory levels. [Transitory levels]
- Record the diagnosis before choosing the hook, mechanism depth, story length, and CTA timing. [Write the page for the state they arrived in (the direct-response VSL school); market sophistication - the direct-response VSL school’s five-stage claim cycle]

### Problem-aware traffic

**State:** They know they have a problem but do not yet realize a credible solution exists. [The five awareness levels]

- Lead with the lived problem and its consequences, not with coaching, the method, the product, or price. [What to say at each of the five stages]
- Make the problem present and specific enough to become “lack on fire,” then show that it is fixable. [Problem state and solution state]
- Delay the CTA until the bottom because the viewer cannot value the solution before the problem-to-solution bridge is complete. [Write the page for the state they arrived in]
- Spend more of the 20 minutes on recognition, causal diagnosis, and possibility; keep mechanism explanation at outcome-led overview depth until interest exists. [Copy length, visibility, and market profile (the direct-response VSL school); mechanism teaching - How much of the mechanism to show]
- Treat high-ticket conversion from mere problem awareness as an exceptional case. This doctrine’s “wall of conversion” says a problem-aware low-ticket buyer is not automatically ready for a $5,000 to $10,000 decision. [The wall of conversion]

### Solution-aware traffic

**State:** They know the problem and generally know the solution category, but do not yet know or prefer this specific offer. [The five awareness levels]

- Name the desire, prove the solution, and introduce the specific vehicle only after showing why it is the right expression of that solution. [What to say at each of the five stages]
- Shorten the problem setup. Cut education about anything they already know. [Know what they already know and Copy length, visibility, and market profile]
- Show **how** the result is possible; do not spend the VSL proving only that the category can work. [In aware and hyper-aware markets, show HOW it is possible]
- Demonstrate enough of the mechanism that the viewer can distinguish it from prior attempts and calculate the trade-offs. [How much of the mechanism to show and Outline the method so they can calculate the trade-offs]
- Lead mature prospects with “why it did not work for you,” not another “why this works” lecture. [Market maturity flips the copy]
- Do not copy a high-status competitor’s two-minute VSL unless this seller owns comparable awareness and status. [Unaware / aware / status ladder]

### What a saturated coaching market forces

- Assume **coaching is a known, crowded category**, then verify the specific niche. The direct-response VSL school’s worked saturation read says everyone teaches coaches, known problems are abundant, and few sellers introduce something genuinely unknown. [known versus unknown]
- Inventory what buyers already know, what they have tried, what failed, and what remains unexplained. Build the VSL around the consequential unknown, not a restated known promise. [known versus unknown]
- Do not compete with a larger version of “grow your coaching business,” “get more clients,” or “scale faster” after direct and enlarged claims have been exhausted. Move to a credible new mechanism or a new segment. [The five stages]
- Make novelty material, not cosmetic. Renaming the same claim without a new explanation, process, or segment is a named failure. [The five stages and Innovation drives every stage]
- In an advanced market, do not attack the foundational mechanism buyers already believe. Challenge the **timeline**: show how the accepted approach used to work and why one component no longer works now. [Challenge the timeline, not the mechanism]
- Use the saturated-coaching timeline opening: easier past → change point → everyone copies the same tactic → the familiar move now arrives too late. [Timeline language in the why-what-how ad opening]
- Drop inflated excitement when the audience is burned. Use sober, direct language and show the work. [Romantic / infatuation / burned / realism (tone) and Irrational to rational to direct (proof type)]
- Shift the offer from more education toward implementation, templates, or done-for-you support when the market has moved from learning to implementation. [Education to done-for-you (offer type)]
- Let credibility and proof displace story as the market becomes more aware; do not compensate for weak product evidence with a longer founder story. [Credibility displaces story]
- Treat application-funnel recognition as part of saturation. If viewers recognize the “free training → application → sales call” trap, test a diagnostic quiz or a genuinely evaluative proposition rather than disguising the same blunt CTA. [Tension - application vs quiz vs T2 vs country gate; The flip over]

### Condition-based ruling where the classic awareness model and the direct-response VSL school diverge

- Use the classic awareness model’s five-stage claim cycle to choose what kind of claim can still land: direct promise, enlarged promise, new mechanism, enlarged mechanism, or new segment. [the direct-response VSL school’s five-stage claim cycle]
- Use the direct-response VSL school’s known/unknown inventory to decide what remains unsaid in today’s individualized internet market. This school explicitly no longer uses the classic awareness model as a literal individual-staging tool because modern markets change too quickly and cannot be grouped as cleanly. [known versus unknown]
- Do not “resolve” the tension by discarding either source. For this VSL, use the five-stage cycle at the **market-claim** level and the known/unknown, journey, tone, offer-type, and proof-type axes at the **current segment** level. This is the condition routing recorded in this doctrine. [condition note; Other maturity axes the direct-response VSL school uses]

### Required brief fields

- Awareness stage at arrival: problem-aware, solution-aware, or a named transitory state.
- Evidence: actual questions, ads consumed, prior purchases, failed attempts, and product/category language used by prospects. [Count the questions and Know what they already know]
- Sophistication read: absorbed claim, absorbed mechanism, known problems, unexplained unknown, market tone, implementation expectation, and proof standard. [The five stages; Four stages of saturation; Other maturity axes the direct-response VSL school uses]
- Resulting VSL decision: opening type, problem minutes, mechanism depth, origin-story length, proof type, and CTA timestamp.

## 5. Mechanism: naming rules and where it lands on the timeline

### Define the object before naming it

- Define the mechanism as the reason the transformation works: the piece, process, system, or causal engine that delivers the primary promise. Do not confuse it with the campaign’s big idea, the product, the community, a benefit such as speed, or a catchy title. [Definition and the two halves (the big-idea school; the direct-response VSL school; a proven teaching); Ruling out, validating, and the not-a-mechanism test]
- Build two linked halves: **UM-Problem**, why prior attempts failed despite reasonable effort, and **UM-Solution**, why this approach can work. The solution half must answer the problem half without a logical jump. [Definition and the two halves]
- Write the causal steps before the copy. Research and prewrite the problem mechanism and solution mechanism before the lead, story polish, or final outline. [Where the mechanism sits in the workflow]
- Require substantiation. The RMBC copy school prefers scientifically or factually valid mechanisms; this doctrine records admitted fabrications as regret or pattern demonstrations, not permission to invent. [How true it has to be]
- Make the mechanism strong enough to transfer blame from the prospect’s character to the missing causal piece without mechanically repeating “it is not your fault.” [Hope, blame, and the missing piece]

### Naming rules

- Use a proprietary name when the generic description collapses into a commodity search. Sell the named process, not “more research,” “coaching,” “a community,” or “a seven-step system” with no owned expression. [proprietary vs vernacular]
- Prefer a short, memorable name the prospect can repeat and point to. Acronyms are permitted when they are simple and meaningful. [proprietary vs vernacular]
- Name the **problem** as specifically as the solution when a generic problem label hides the missing causal piece. [proprietary vs vernacular]
- Use the market’s vernacular instead of forcing a proprietary label when the term is still new, already desired, and immediately understood. The big-idea school’s vernacular-naming exception used “traffic and conversion” because the market already believed those were the answer. [proprietary vs vernacular]
- Translate cold-market jargon into an image or desired effect. “Evergreen” can mean nothing to a cold viewer; express the outcome in language they use. [proprietary vs vernacular]
- Bring a familiar but abstract process to life with a concrete metaphor, while remaining accurate. [proprietary vs vernacular (the big-idea school); Metaphor, grade level, and proof]
- In coaching, do not pretend an ordinary method is technologically novel. This doctrine says coaching mechanisms are often honestly simple: a distinctive name for a known problem or process can be enough when the distinguishing difference is real. [AI workflows and nickname markets]
- Run the not-a-mechanism test: ask what actually produces the result. If the answer is not the named thing, rename or rebuild it. [Ruling out, validating, and the not-a-mechanism test]

### Name it, count it, walk it, state the miss

### I-I-I cue and control test

Apply both tests to every raw objection before scripting:

1. **Pronoun cue:** If the objection is phrased around "I," provisionally mark it internal.
2. **Control test:** Ask whether the prospect has some control over the named condition.
3. **Internal ruling:** Some control means internal, even when the issue is wealth, time allocation, confidence, knowledge, or resources.
4. **External ruling:** No meaningful control means external. Timing is the model external condition.
5. **One-slot rule:** Put each objection in exactly one slot. Do not classify by topic alone.
6. **Override rule:** The control test decides when the pronoun cue and the subject matter appear to conflict.

- "I do not have enough experience" is internal because capability can be developed.
- "I do not have time" begins as internal under the I-I-I cue; inspect what portion of the schedule is controllable before final placement.

- "This will not happen for someone like me" may be external when it means uncontrollable timing or circumstance, but must be rewritten precisely before sorting.

- Use a **Total Business Cap** only when the service genuinely limits the number of active clients at that delivery level. State the cap or the availability accurately. [Real capacity is the safest scarcity]
- Use a **Growth Rate Cap** only when onboarding or delivery genuinely limits new clients per week or month. Exact shape: “only X new clients per week,” with X sourced from operations. [Real capacity is the safest scarcity]
- Use a **cohort cap** only when the cohort has a real seat limit or facilitator-to-client constraint. Do not call unlimited webinar seats coaching capacity. [Real capacity is the safest scarcity]
- Use one-to-one access as scarcity only when a named expert’s calendar is genuinely the bottleneck. [Real capacity is the safest scarcity]
- If demand exceeds capacity, use a waitlist or honestly add declared capacity. Do not keep taking bookings under a supposedly exhausted cap. [Someone must miss out; The double-edge of quantity caps]
- Make the call-level line specific and checkable: number of qualified calls accepted per week, number of new coaching starts, or available cohort seats. Never use “a few spots” when nobody knows the number. [Real capacity is the safest scarcity]

### Allowed timing urgency

- Use a fixed cohort date when the group actually starts together. The exact wording shape in this doctrine is: “sign up today, the next group starts Monday, otherwise wait a week.” Adapt only the verified date and wait period. [Cohort and rolling-start urgency]
- Use a soft cutoff when timing is approximate but availability is truly limited; use a hard date when the opportunity actually changes or closes on that date. [hard deadline vs soft cutoff]
- Prefer a dated statement over vague “soon” language when the date exists. State timezone where a deadline affects booking. [hard deadline vs soft cutoff]
- Enforce the aftermath. If a cohort or booking window closes, the destination must become closed, full, or waitlist; nothing-changing-at-zero destroys integrity. [Launch and evergreen mechanics; the deadline's aftermath]
- Let near-term scheduling support attendance: high-ticket calls should normally be bookable inside 48 hours to 3 days, but do not misrepresent calendar configuration as scarcity. [Recency, lag, and interference]

### CTA wording constraints

- State the action directly: book, choose a time, complete the form. Pair it with the real reason to do it now. [The consensus claims, in full]
- Use loss framing only around a true lost opportunity: missing this cohort means waiting until the verified next start, or a filled call slot means that slot is no longer available. [Loss framing over gain framing]
- Do not invent personal catastrophe as pressure. “Cost of delay” must describe the prospect’s existing trajectory, not guarantee that a dramatic loss will occur. [Loss framing over gain framing; the sales-copy checks M8 Truth guardrail]
- Close on the high/solution state: action is the first step toward the wanted outcome, not surrender to panic. [Emotional momentum / emotional wave]

## 11. Script-for-speech rules: words become images, cadence/emotional wave, cognitive load, over-explaining, sentence length for spoken delivery, single voice.

### Words become images

- Write for the image produced in the listener’s mind. If a phrase cannot be pictured, replace the abstraction with a truthful moment, place, object, action, sensation, or visible contrast. [Words must become images]
- Treat “save time,” “reduce stress,” “grow faster,” “get clarity,” and similar labels as incomplete until the script shows what they look like in lived experience. [the sales-copy checks M7 Concrete agitation]
- Let the picture carry the concept before explanation. Show then tell; do not force the listener to build an image from a technical wall. [What to put on the page]
- Use one visual per required belief where possible. The image must be immediately digestible and captioned with its proof job. [What to put on the page]
- Use process artifacts, demonstrations, and before/after contrasts to turn intangible coaching into something visible. [What to put on the page]

### Emotional wave

- Diagnose the audience’s starting state before setting the wave. Problem/low state: low → high → low → high. Solution/high state: high → low → high. Always close on the high. [Emotional momentum / emotional wave]
- Use **why/when** to activate problem/away states and **what/how** to activate solution/toward states. Do not ask for the call while the viewer is still in the problem state. [Emotional momentum / emotional wave]
- Make the CTA the first step inside the solution state. The close should feel like movement toward the wanted outcome, not a scolding. [Emotional momentum / emotional wave]
- Avoid a flat emotional line. Alternate tension and release, but do not manufacture events or inflate consequences. [Emotional momentum / emotional wave; the sales-copy checks M8 Truth guardrail]
- Write the last line of every beat deliberately. First and last images remain crisp; the middle blurs. [Last-thing-said rule]

### Cadence and sentence length

- Match sentence length to emotional state: high emotion gets short, “chop-chop” sentences; low emotion gets longer, drawn-out sentences. [Cadence in copy]
- Use short spoken lines for impact, contrast, reveal, and the high-state CTA. Do not keep every line short; uninterrupted chop prevents rapport and image formation. [Cadence in copy; Tonality and cadence]
- Use longer sentences only when the listener needs to inhabit a low-state scene or follow one causal chain. Break the sentence if it carries more than one idea, mechanism, or direction. [Cadence in copy; One idea, one concept, one thought, one mechanism at a time]
- Start with higher energy and conscious attention, settle into medium cadence for rapport, then lower pitch before lowering cadence so the listener does more of the processing. [pitch down, then cadence down]
- Do not use fast cadence and high pitch during rapport. The listener cannot form images quickly enough to connect. [Tonality and cadence]
- Resolve the apparent cadence conflict by condition: short sentences express high emotional pitch; slower delivery builds rapport and feeling. Do not translate “short sentence” into “rush the read.” [Tonality and cadence]
- Set delivery pace to the audience’s processing speed; the direct-response VSL school’s named route is slower for older audiences and faster for younger audiences. [Modulate pace, music, accent and voice to match the audience]

### Cognitive load and explanation

- Cap the number of distinct points active in a beat. The direct-response VSL school’s bound is seven plus or minus two: about five in a low emotional state, up to nine in a high state. [Seven plus or minus two]
- Prefer depth on one thing over breadth across many. Flat copy often contains too many points and no dimensionalized center. [Give copy dimension]
- State one idea, concept, thought, or mechanism at a time. Do not make the listener sort parallel frameworks while watching. [One idea, one concept, one thought, one mechanism at a time]
- Mix questions, statements, and stories. Statement-only delivery invites true/false judgment and polarization; uncontrolled questions can invite answers the script cannot absorb. [answers questions, judges statements, listens to stories]
- Treat over-explaining as a diagnosis, not an editing symptom. It means the writer does not yet understand the offer, mechanism, or audience. Return to research before cutting words. [Over-explaining]
- Explain enough to create possibility and attainability, then move. A mechanism that requires endless description is harder to sell; demonstration compresses it. [Insight → Interest → Intent → Action; claim-proof pairing What to put on the page]
- Remove any sentence that educates without increasing insight, interest, intent, proof, fit, or action. Length exists to build intent, not to display knowledge. [the attention/intent equilibrium]

### Single voice

- Choose one face and one lead. A corporation has no voice; people buy from people. [Single voice, single face, single lead]
- Write in the speaker’s translated natural voice, not generic direct-response voice and not the writer’s impersonation of the speaker. [Single voice, single face, single lead]
- Tell one person’s story at a time. Do not merge multiple customer histories into an unmarked composite or say “everybody.” [Single voice, single face, single lead; the sales-copy checks M8 Truth guardrail]
- Build the VSL around the actual speaker and offer. Do not finish a generic script and cast a person into it afterward. [Build the VSL around the person, not the person into the VSL]
- Run a live read-through. At every stumble, ask how the speaker would say it, replace the line with those words, and read it again. [rewrite to the expert’s own cadence]
- Preserve the speaker’s recurring vocabulary and sentence patterns while enforcing truth, proof, and the approved never-say list. Natural cadence does not license factual drift. [Avoid language rigidity in a conversational format; the sales-copy checks M8 Truth guardrail]

### Ruling protocol

1. Put each selected condition in the signed brief before the writer begins.
2. Treat unselected branches as nonrequirements, not silent failures.
3. Test unresolved variants only after the default version passes every MUST gate.
4. Change one disputed variable per test and measure qualified booked calls, show rate, and accepted-client rate.
5. Preserve the result with traffic source, device mix, offer, date, and sample size; never promote a local winner into universal doctrine.
