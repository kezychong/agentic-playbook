# Intake: Mining the Raw Material

The intake is where the webinar is actually won. Every high-converting element downstream - headline, pain section, stories, objection closes - is assembled from what you extract here. Live intake sessions from the source systems show the pattern: the subject's own dramatic specifics become the pitch copy nearly verbatim (one flagship webinar's "[verified result] in [verified period]" intake answer became the literal webinar headline).

**Working rule: extract before you ask.** Read every raw material the user provides (offer docs, sales pages, past webinars, testimonials, audience research) and pre-fill the brief. Then ask only for gaps, in conversational phases - never a wall of 30 questions.

## The Client Brief (target state)

| Field | What you need |
|-------|---------------|
| Niche & audience | Who exactly; what problem; where they hang out online |
| Offer | What's sold; type (info / SaaS / service / high-ticket); price; delivery format; 3 key benefits |
| Webinar format | Live or automated; duration; CTA goal (direct purchase vs. application/call) |
| Voice & tone | Formal/casual, technical/accessible, high-energy/measured; brand references |
| Top 5 objections | Why the audience would NOT buy (topic-level - see Field 16 rule below) |
| Origin story beats | The presenter's before-state, wall, epiphany, conflict, result (see Phase 2) |
| Proof inventory | Specific numbers, client results, testimonials, screenshots, endorsements |
| Existing assets | Past webinars, sales pages, ad creative, brand guidelines |

## The Master Intake Framework (5 Phases)

Merged from the Close-First school's audience-psychology research system and the Story-Led school's live narrative-extraction interviews. Run the phases as a conversation; each phase's answers feed specific webinar sections.

### Phase 1: Market & Identity (feeds: routing, Hook, Positioning)

- Who is this for, and who is it explicitly NOT for?
- What do they call themselves? What do they call the problem? (Use their words, not marketing-speak.)
- Is the buying decision emotional (B2C/opportunity) or analytical (B2B/tool)? → drives framework routing.
- What has this audience already been pitched? (What are they numb to?)

### Phase 2: Origin Story Extraction (feeds: Intro origin story, offer reason-why)

Interview for the 8-Step Story Arc beats. Push past the polished version - the value is in the mess:

- "Where were you before this worked? Paint the worst moment." (backstory + wall)
- "What did you want on the surface - and why did that matter to you, really?" Ask "why?" repeatedly (5-8 times) until you hit the internal desire (love, acceptance, proving worth). External achievements create distance ("good for you"); internal transformation creates identification ("that's me").
- "What did you try that failed? How much did it cost you?" (old vehicle - get numbers)
- "What was the exact moment you realized the new way?" (epiphany - get sensory detail: where were you, what did you see/feel)
- "What almost made you quit after that?" (conflict - no conflict, no story)
- "What's the specific result, and who are you now that you weren't before?" (achievement + transformation)

### Phase 3: Product & Offer Audit (feeds: Offer, Stack)

- Everything included, itemized. Which pieces are tangible tools (templates, scripts, software) vs. more training? Tools carry the stack; training weighs it down.
- What could be ADDED as bonuses? Probe for done-for-you pieces, communities, coaching, licenses (see modality tiers in sections-close.md).
- What can be genuinely scarce or time-limited - and what's the true reason why?
- Price, and what the audience currently pays for inferior alternatives.

### Phase 4: Pain & False Beliefs (feeds: Pain section, Content secrets, closes)

Fill the 16-field Buyer Profile (below). Where the user doesn't know their audience well enough, assign research (sources below) before generating - never fabricate audience psychology.

### Phase 5: Proof, Naming & Headline (feeds: Positioning, headline, testimonial clusters)

- Collect every provable number, ranked by drama. Specificity converts: "$14,700 in 30 days" beats "great results".
- Name the method: the mechanism needs a 1-2 word ownable label (Category of One positioning).
- Draft the headline with the extraction formula: **[Specific Dramatic Proof] + [Promise of Replication] + [Even If Qualifier]** - "How I made [verified result] in [verified period] - and how you can copy the system, even if nobody knows who you are."

## The 16-Field Buyer Profile

The audience-psychology instrument that feeds Pain, Gain, story shaping, and close selection. One line each:

1. **Avatar basics** - name, age, one-paragraph description of a single representative person
2. **Core challenge/problem** - the one problem the webinar addresses
3. **Five most powerful emotions** - what they feel about the problem (not what they think)
4. **Five biggest fears** - ranked; fuels Fear/Risk closes
5. **Five ways fears affect relationships** - spouse, kids, colleagues (the dinner-table lens)
6. **Hurtful things others say** - actual quotes they hear ("when are you going to get a real job?")
7. **Five failed solutions** - what they tried that didn't work; feeds close #35: Identify the missing condition
8. **Soundbites about failures** - how they describe the failures in their own words
9. **What they refuse to do** - hard boundaries (cold calling, video, paid ads); respect these in the offer
10. **Soundbites about refusals** - their phrasing of the refusals
11. **Perfect solution outcomes** - magic-wand answers; fuels Gain section
12. **How success affects relationships** - positive AND negative (fear of outgrowing peers is real)
13. **What success hinges upon** - what they believe is the key missing piece
14. **What they must give up** - perceived sacrifices; pre-handle in content
15. **Who they blame** - align with their blame frame, never fight it (check whether a missing resource contributed to the setback)
16. **Biggest objections - TOPIC-level only.** Objections to the topic/category ("webinars are dead", "I'm not a salesperson"), NOT to your product. Product objections belong to the close section; topic objections must be handled in the Hook and Content. Mixing these corrupts both sections.

## Research Sources (when audience knowledge is thin)

Mine the audience's own language - the Pain Mirror only works with mundane, specific detail in their words:

- Reddit threads + Facebook groups in the niche (complaint posts, "am I the only one who..." posts)
- Amazon reviews of competing books/programs (1-star = pains and objections; 5-star = desired outcomes)
- YouTube comments on competitor content; sales-call recordings and support tickets if available
- A proven research prompt: "Pretend you're my friend and we're having a drink. Vent to me about all the frustrations, pains, and challenges you have when it comes to [topic]." Then drill any answer with Five Whys - ask "why does that bother you?" five levels down until you reach the identity-level pain (surface: "I hate my accounting" → core: "I'm terrified I'm not actually cut out to run a business").

## Offer-Type Adaptations (apply across all phases)

| Offer type | Intake emphasis |
|---|---|
| Info product | Transformation stories, identity language, before/after specifics |
| SaaS | Demo-able moments, workflow pains, time/money quantification, competitor tools |
| Services | Authority markers, case studies, trust objections ("will this work for MY situation") |
| High-ticket | Deeper origin story, exclusivity rationale, application criteria, strongest proof |
