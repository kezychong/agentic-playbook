# Sections 4-7: Transition, Offer, Stack, Close (45-90+ min)

This half of the webinar is where the money is made - the Close-First school scripts it FIRST (priority: Close > Intro > Transition > Content) and most revenue arrives after the first CTA. Never let it be the rushed afterthought.

---

## Section 4: TRANSITION (45-48 min)

Job: pivot from learning mode to buying mode without triggering "here comes the pitch" resistance. Three minutes that decide whether the offer feels inevitable or ambush-like. Bonus effect: it manages the PRESENTER's psychology too - the recap re-convinces you of the value you just delivered right when pitch anxiety peaks.

### Default: the 3-Step Protocol (write it LAST, after content + offer exist)

**Step 1 - Recap (~60 sec).** 5-7 emotionally charged takeaways from the content, escalating in impressiveness, delivered bang-bang-bang. Phrase as transformation, not information: "You discovered how to..." - never "I taught you...". Scale with price: 5 bullets for a $97 offer, 8-12 for $1,000+. Close the recap with an open loop: "and we've only scratched the surface." Why: the audience has consciously forgotten most of the content; the recap triggers a "we covered SO much" gratitude spike that makes the offer feel proportional.

**Step 2 - Tie-Down Cascade (4-6 questions, ~60 sec).** Move from usefulness to fit to interest in support. Six questions is a rehearsal target; leave room for honest disagreement.
- Easy: "Which idea could you use this week?" / "Was the example relevant to your work?"
- Medium: "Where might this approach fit your routine?" / "Would a small test be practical for you?"
- Big + commitment bridge: "Could you make room to practice this consistently?" → "Would guided support help you begin?"

**Step 3 - Two Choices (~60 sec).** Compare self-directed practice with supported implementation. Use an original invitation grounded in what the offer actually includes:

> "You can practice today's lesson independently. If you prefer feedback while you apply it, I can explain the coaching option and what support it includes. Would that be useful? The program is [product name]."

The psychology: acknowledge independent progress as a valid option, explain the added support, invite interest, then introduce the product. Respect the solo route and avoid implying that buying is the only way to succeed.

### Alternative: the Permission Question (Story-Led school; charismatic presenters, warm audiences only)

> "Would you like a brief look at the paid support available for applying this lesson? In a live session, tell me whether that would help. I'll explain the scope, fee and who should pass."

Allow 2-3 minutes if the audience needs time to reflect. Acknowledge complexity and invite questions without inventing chat activity. If nobody responds, explain the offer briefly and give people a clear option to leave.

**Mistakes:** abrupt pivot with no bridge; skipping the recap (kills perceived value); apologizing for selling ("I hate this part, but..."); belittling the go-alone option.

## Section 5: OFFER REVEAL + PRICE (48-55 min)

Job: present the vehicle so it collapses the gap between "I learned something valuable" and "here's what delivers the result."

### Offer intro origin story (30-60 sec, not a full 8-Step Story Arc)

For the short origin story, explain the original difficulty, the experiment you tried, the method you developed and any documented experience helping others. Then explain why you made the offer. Do not invent faster client results or a reluctant-founder story.

### Single product → One Thing Flow

1. Story (above) → 2. Reason why → 3. **One-Sentence Overview**: "The [offer type] supports [specific outcome] through [method], with [actual delivery schedule]." If you can't say it in one sentence, the offer is confused - fix the offer, not the sentence. → 4. **Coolest Parts**: 3-7 components, 2 min max each, each via the Element Formula (Headline + 3 benefits + proof + future pacing). → 5. **Access**: platform, format, timing - concrete logistics collapse the distance between "considering" and "using."

### Multi-component → Keys To Success Flow

Name the three main resources required for the outcome and explain each role. Present benefits, evidence, practical use and relevant differences for each resource, allowing no more than 2 minutes per item. Then connect them: "These pieces support one workflow; which part would make the biggest difference for you?" State the price. Aim for 3 core deliverables, with a maximum of 4; classify other items as bonuses.

### High-ticket / live → the Stack build (Story-Led school)

Six elements in order, each mapped to a belief: (1) core training → vehicle; (2) tools/software → vehicle; (3) tangible bonus → vehicle false belief; (4) tangible → internal false belief; (5) tangible → external false belief; (6) exclusive scarcity bonus → urgency. The offer isn't designed to "add value" - it systematically destroys every remaining objection. Before adding anything ask: "which false belief does this destroy?"

**Setup-Payoff-Tie-Down** can take 30 seconds to 3 minutes per item: describe the problem, show the resource, then check its value. Example: "Would solving that one bottleneck justify the fee for you?" This uses supplementary close S14 in `closes.md`.

**Tangible tools over training:** templates, scripts, checklists, and software out-pull programs. More training signals "more work for you"; tools signal "here's the shortcut." The best-performing stack item on record was a two-page phone script - a compact tool can be more immediately useful than another lengthy program.

### Price reveal: the routed decision

**Early reveal (default, digital under $5K - Close-First school):** price lands within the first 3-5 minutes of the offer section, THEN bonuses stack. The audience knows the cost, so every subsequent bonus makes refusing feel increasingly irrational. Use the **Four-Tier Price Descent** to anchor: what it's worth to you personally → what it's earned clients → what competitors charge → website price → today's special price. (Non-monetizable niches: anchor on cost-of-development instead of ROI.)

**Delayed reveal (high-ticket $5K+ / live stage - Story-Led school):**
- **Price Marinade** - introduce a clearly labeled value anchor in the first 15-20 minutes: "As we review the package, compare its usefulness with [supported value anchor]." The anchor must match the final stated stack value. Distinguish assigned value from the actual price; never imply a fictitious retail price.
- **If/All Statements** - after showing the stack, use three benefit checks, one for each belief category. Example: "Would achieving [specific benefit] make this package worth considering at [value anchor]?" Ask for a real assessment rather than presumed agreement.
- **Price Drop** - distinguish the anchor from the fee: "That comparison helps explain the value; the amount due is [actual price]." Use one clear reveal unless further comparisons have a specific purpose. Keep the checkout URL visible afterward.

**Mistakes:** feature lists instead of outcomes (nobody buys "8 modules"); apologetic pricing ("Sorry about the fee..." - signals the price isn't justified); price with no anchor (sticker shock); more than 7 core components (confusion, not desire); skipping access logistics.

## Section 6: BONUS STACK + GUARANTEE + SCARCITY (55-75 min)

Job: convert "interested" into "buying." Most of the audience is a no at the price reveal - bonuses, guarantee, and scarcity systematically flip them. Core principle: bonuses can address reasons the core package alone has not resolved - spend MORE time selling bonuses than the core product (each major bonus gets its own 2-3 minute mini-pitch).

### Bonus selection

Every bonus must do at least one of: (1) destroy a specific remaining objection (belief-mapping), (2) add a different modality than the core offer (the diversity rule), (3) serve a specific segment or speed need. Random "extra value" bonuses are filler that weakens the stack.

**Modality tiers by perceived value** - include at least one S or A tier:
- **S:** done-for-you, software, 1-on-1 coaching, reseller license, live events
- **A:** community, group coaching, critiques, templates, certification
- **B:** swipe files, trainings, case studies, support, AI prompts, over-the-shoulder
- **C:** PDFs, audio, transcripts (baseline - never lead with these)

Useful economics: outsourced group-coaching bonuses typically redeem at a fraction (25-50%) of buyers, so a high-perceived-value coaching bonus costs a small fraction of face value per buyer. Done-for-you design: start from "Which tasks could we take off their hands?" and subtract until feasible - even doing ONE step for them (or a tool that does it) keeps DFY-level perceived value.

### Sequencing (cascades from price timing)

Early price reveal → save the BEST bonus for last (bonuses are the conversion mechanism; maximum tension gets the strongest push). Delayed price → lead with the strongest element (anchor high early). Always: open strong, close strong, alternate modalities (never two of the same type back-to-back), vary emotional appeal (sexy → lasting value → instant gratification → proof), include one mystery bonus, minimum 3, sweet spot 7, past 8 = cognitive overload.

### The Delete-From-Recording bonus (the extended-close engine)

The final, most dramatic bonus - a genuinely webinar-only exclusive, revealed theatrically:

> "This bonus is available to qualifying enrollments received by [real deadline]. The written terms at [offer URL] explain eligibility and how you receive it."

For a genuine live-only bonus, explain the documented eligibility window. When someone reports a checkout problem, offer practical help without naming them unless they consent. Example: "A buyer has asked for help with enrollment; I can address questions while that is resolved." Do not prolong or fabricate scarcity.

### Guarantee (position AFTER core + price + bonuses: remove risk only once value is fully built)

| Type | Shape | When |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | "The written policy gives you [refund window] to request repayment through [contact method]." | New offers with a genuine refund period |
| Conditional | "A refund requires [documented action] by [deadline]; review those conditions before enrolling." | Offers with clear, reasonable participation requirements |
| Better-than-money-back | "If the published criteria are met, the policy provides a refund plus [specified compensation]." | Only when the business can honor the documented promise |
| All sales final | "This purchase is non-refundable; check suitability and ask your questions before paying." | Offers with a lawful, clearly disclosed final-sale policy |

Explain the policy prominently, including exclusions, deadlines and the request process. For a genuine trial, use close #40: Evaluate within real terms, in `closes.md`. A refund promise does not make every purchase affordable or suitable.

### Scarcity (seeded throughout, concentrated here)

Four types - combine at least two: deadline (price/offer expires), bonus-based (Delete-From-Recording), price-based (founding-member pricing), quantity/access ("only taking 17 to maintain quality"). **Every scarcity claim needs a reason why** ("I reward fast action-takers" is the minimum viable reason; a third-party contract limiting distribution - shown on screen - is the gold standard). Never fake it: fake scarcity is discovered, and it destroys trust permanently. Frame with Pain of Regret: the cost of NOT acting (staying stuck, watching others pass them) must weigh more than the cost of acting.

**Trial closes go explicit here**: follow each bonus or testimonial with relevant reflection. Examples: "Where would you use that resource?" and "Which part of this example matches your situation?" Prepare 4-6 checks after major proof, but avoid mechanical repetition. Between items ask, "What support would still be missing?"

## Section 7: CLOSE + CTA WAVES (75-90 min)

Job: convert the undecided with rotating objection-handling waves. The first CTA captures only the hot buyers; the extended close converts the remaining 60-80% of potential buyers. Read `closes.md` for the full close library, trial-close system, and the Q&A Close section (Section 8).

### The wave structure

Rotate objection TYPES - never hammer one objection from three angles in a row:

1. **Wave 1 - Hot buyers:** direct invitation → Live A: Welcome with permission, only for verified purchases → genuine fast-action terms.
2. **Wave 2 - Price:** #21: Price the delay → relevant documented value evidence → invitation with any actual payment-plan terms.
3. **Wave 3 - Fear/risk:** #18: Give success equal attention → relevant evidence from initially skeptical clients → policy reminder.
4. **Wave 4 - Identity/belief:** #20: Retest the old limit, or #45: Begin before you're polished → real beginner examples → invitation.
5. **Wave 5 - Final scarcity push:** explain any true capacity limit, cutoff or bonus expiry → compare supported and independent routes using S7 → finish with a clear action and a large, readable URL.

Testimonial selection rule: each cluster covers a DIFFERENT segment (experience level, niche, result type, timeline) so every remaining viewer sees someone like them. Real screenshots beat designed graphics.

### Extended close mechanics (Close-First school)

Keep the offer and its link visible while answering questions. Establish the underlying concern without assuming it, answer the factual question, then use the matching technique from `closes.md` if helpful. Do not require a purchase to receive information needed for the decision. State genuine deadlines accurately. Example: "Let's settle your access question before you decide whether to enroll."

End with a clear invitation and accurate terms. Example: "Before we finish, review the enrollment details at [offer URL] and decide whether this is your next step." Mention a closing deadline only if the offer really changes then.

### High-ticket variant: the 4-Question Close (Story-Led school, $5K+ application offers)

The webinar drives to an application/call instead of a cart. On the call, four questions - the prospect sells themselves:

1. "Which changes would make a year of working together feel worthwhile to you?" Ask what those changes mean personally.
2. "What has prevented progress so far, and which parts could we realistically address?" Distinguish changeable barriers from constraints the offer cannot solve.
3. "Which strengths or resources could you put to better use with support?" Look for a practical starting point.
4. "Would working together on that plan suit you?" Pause and let the person answer without filling the silence.

## Context: the funnel around the webinar

The script is one piece of a machine (the classic weekly model): registration page → thank-you page (optional $37-47 self-liquidating offer) → indoctrination videos → reminder emails → live webinar → **replay emails Fri-Sun (replays roughly double total sales)** → cart close Sunday midnight (urgency spike). The classic $997 webinar math: ~$3/registrant ad cost, ~25% show rate, ~10% close of attendees. A webinar may need repeated live revisions before ever automating it - do live reps before automating, and expect the numbers to come from iteration, not the first run.
