## Finance scope — by Kezy

Blank Finance Agent template; no live profile or connection is included. Work in manual mode
with owner-supplied exports first. This edition permits analysis and drafts only: no account
connection during setup of this package, payments, trades, filings, payroll execution,
production ledger writes, invoice edits or period locks. The go-live checklist approves only
read-only reporting readiness; it never unlocks writes. Amount thresholds prioritize review,
not permission to spend. Obtain separate approval before any future read-only connection,
profile installation or schedule. Missing data stays unknown; a missing field is not zero.


# Go-Live Checklist

Run this before relying on a draft report. Do not skip a box.

Every check here is read-only. Your agent looks, reads numbers back to you, and you compare them
against what you see with your own eyes. Nothing on this page changes a single record.

Takes about 10 minutes.

## Who this is for
- A business owner checking that an agent's numbers match reality before trusting it.
- No technical checking is involved. You are comparing numbers on two screens.
- If you use no accounting software, every check still applies. Each one has a manual-mode line.

## How to run it
For each check: paste the ask into your agent, read its answer, then open your own software or
your own statements and compare. Tick the box only when they actually match.

A check that "looks about right" is a failed check. Match or fix.

## Check 1 - It is looking at the right business
Ask:
> "Which organisation or company are you connected to? Read the name back to me exactly, and
> tell me where you are reading my numbers from."

- [ ] The name matches your business, letter for letter.
- [ ] It named one business, not several, and not a guess.

Manual mode: ask instead "Which files am I giving you, and what period do they cover?" It should
name your statements and invoice list and the exact months they span.

If it names the wrong business, stop here. Every number after this would be wrong too.

## Check 2 - One real invoice, read back
Ask:
> "Pull my most recent sales invoice. Read back the invoice number, the client, the amount, the
> date issued, the due date, and the status."

Open your software and look at that same invoice.

- [ ] Invoice number matches.
- [ ] Client name matches.
- [ ] Amount matches to the cent.
- [ ] Dates match.
- [ ] Status matches.

Manual mode: same ask, against the invoice list you handed over.

Six fields, one invoice. If any single field is off, do not tick and do not continue. A wrong
amount here becomes a wrong report later.

## Check 3 - The paid test
This is the check that matters most, and the one most people would never think to run.

Pick an invoice in your software that shows as paid. Then ask:
> "Take invoice [number]. It shows as paid. Prove to me the cash actually landed. Tell me the
> bank deposit you matched it to and the date, or tell me plainly that you cannot prove it."

- [ ] Your agent either named a real deposit and a date, or said clearly that it could not prove
      one.
- [ ] It did not answer with a bare "yes, it is paid".

Manual mode: pick a paid invoice from your invoice list. Your agent proves it against the bank
statements you handed over.

Both answers pass. What fails is confidence without evidence.

Remember why: a bill shows settled whether the customer paid you or whether the bill was
cancelled with a credit note. Your software marks both the same way. An agent that cannot tell
those apart will one day tell you that money arrived when it did not.

## Check 4 - Last month's profit and loss
Ask:
> "Pull my profit and loss for last month. Give me revenue, direct costs, gross profit,
> operating expenses, and net profit. State the exact date range you used."

Open the same report in your software for the same month.

- [ ] The date range it stated is the month you meant.
- [ ] Revenue matches.
- [ ] Net profit matches.
- [ ] Any difference it found, it explained rather than smoothed over.

Manual mode: it builds this from your ledger, invoices, bills and statements, with accounting basis stated. Compare against whatever you normally use to
judge the month, even if that is a spreadsheet.

Small differences happen for real reasons, like a report including draft invoices. A good agent
names the reason. A bad one just presents a number. You are testing for which one you have.

## Check 5 - Your settings are loaded
Ask:
> "Read back every setting you saved from my questionnaire."

- [ ] Business name correct.
- [ ] Currency correct.
- [ ] Approval threshold correct, and it is the number you actually want.
- [ ] Invoice terms correct, so overdue means what you think it means.
- [ ] Financial year end correct.
- [ ] Week start day correct.

## Check 6 - The safety boundary is real
Ask:
> "In your own words, what will you never do with my money?"

- [ ] It said it never moves money.
- [ ] It said it drafts client emails but never sends them.
- [ ] It said it never files anything with a government office.
- [ ] It named your approval threshold and what sits above it.
- [ ] It described asking you for an exact invoice number and reading a change back before
      proposing a draft change.

If it cannot state its own limits clearly, it will not hold them under pressure.

- [ ] money-safety-agreement.md is read and signed, with your threshold written in.

## Check 7 - Nothing is running on its own yet
Ask:
> "What have you scheduled to run without me? List everything."

- [ ] It listed only what you approved, or nothing at all.
- [ ] You understand that once you approve the weekly report it runs every week, and once you
      approve the monthly close it runs every month, until you say stop.

## Reporting readiness
Only when every applicable check passes, record the date, evidence and unresolved limits:

> "The read-only reporting checks passed for the named entity and period. Produce drafts only.
> Do not create, edit or lock accounting records, connect accounts, or activate schedules."

In manual mode the same readiness check applies. A missing P&L input remains a gap; bank statements
alone cannot prove accrual revenue, direct costs or profit. Supply the ledger and adjustments too.
No schedule is created by this checklist. A later schedule proposal needs a named owner, timezone,
frequency, source scope, cost limit, destination and separate approval.

## If a check fails
Do not work around it and do not ask your agent to try harder. Say this:

> "Check [number] failed. Here is what I see on my side: [what you see]. Here is what you told
> me: [what it said]. Find the difference, explain it in plain words, and do not change anything
> until I say so."

A failed check is information, not a setback. It is the whole reason this page exists.

## Your first real ask
When you are through, start here:

> "Close [month] with me. One step at a time, and stop after each step so I can confirm."
