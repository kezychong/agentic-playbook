# Money-Safety Agreement

Read this in full. Sign it before your agent does anything real with your books.

This is a gate, not a formality. It sets the one boundary the whole pack is built around: your
agent brings you numbers and recommendations, and you are the only one who moves money.

## Who this is for
- The person who owns the money decisions in this business. That is you.
- No technical knowledge is needed to read or sign this. Every line is about money, not machines.
- Sign it once. It then holds for every conversation you ever have with your agent.

## The core promise
Your agent never moves money. Not now, not after go-live, not once you trust it, not in an
emergency, not because someone asked nicely.

It has no permission to do it. Technical enforcement depends on the granted connector scopes; a written rule alone is not an access control. If you ever see it offer to pay something,
that is a fault, and you should stop and tell it so.

## Four things it will never do
These four are absolute. They do not unlock. There is no threshold that turns them on and no
standing rule that permits them.

1. **Move money.** No payments, no transfers, no refunds, no payroll runs, no card charges.
2. **Send an email itself.** It drafts your invoice emails and your overdue chasers, then hands
   them to you. Your finger presses send, every time, forever.
3. **File anything with a government office.** No tax returns, no filings, no submissions. It can
   prepare and organise. A qualified human files.
4. **Set up a new vendor or supplier payment on its own.** It can prepare the details. You create
   the payee.

## What it does freely, no permission needed
Only within the expressly approved source scope, your agent may:
- Read invoices, payments, bills, cash position, profit and loss.
- Build reports: the weekly money report, the monthly close, the profit and cashflow statements.
- Flag what is overdue, what is odd, what is missing.
- Recommend what it thinks you should do, with the evidence attached.

## What it drafts but never sends
- Invoice emails to clients.
- Overdue payment chasers.
- Any message that goes to a human outside your business.

It writes them. It shows you. You send them. This one does not change after go-live either.

## Your approval threshold
Expenses at or above this amount receive a prominent review flag. No amount authorizes execution:

> Threshold: ____________ (suggested starting point: 100 in your currency)

Below the threshold, your agent still reports every expense in your weekly report. It just does
not stop and ask you first.

You can change this number any time by telling your agent. It takes effect immediately.

## Things that always need your yes, whatever the amount
Amount does not matter for these. They are approved one at a time, every time:
- Anything touching payroll.
- Anything touching tax.
- Anything touching a loan or a credit line.
- Setting up a new vendor or supplier.
- Signing or committing to a contract.

Your yes means you act, or your agent prepares the details for you. It still never executes these
itself.

## Before your agent proposes an invoice change
An invoice is a legal record of what someone owes you. Changing one by accident is a real
problem, and "I thought you meant the other one" is how it happens.

Before drafting a proposed edit, void, paid status or credit note for a human to apply, verify:

1. **You gave the exact invoice number.** Not "the latest one", not "the one for the design job".
   The number.
2. **You said exactly what to change.** Which field, and to what.
3. **Your agent read it back and you confirmed.** It says something like:
   "INV-014 currently shows 2,400 unpaid, due 12 March. You want it marked paid on 9 March.
   Confirm?" You answer. Only then does it finalize the draft recommendation; the human applies any change.

If you ask vaguely, your agent looks the invoice up and asks you a question. It never guesses
which invoice you meant. A guess here is a wrong number in your books.

## The paid rule
Your agent will never tell you an invoice is simply "paid".

A bill can show as settled because a customer paid you, or because the bill was cancelled with a
credit note. In your software both look identical: amount due zero, status paid. One of those
means money is in your bank. The other means it never will be.

So your agent always says one of two things:
- "Paid, cash received on [date]."
- "Credit noted, no cash received."

And before it says the first one, it checks the invoice record for credit notes and looks for a
matching deposit in your bank. If it cannot prove the deposit, it tells you it cannot prove it,
instead of rounding up to good news.

## What your agent never does with your secrets
- Never asks you to type a password or a connection key into a chat.
- Never writes one into working files or reports; use an approved secure credential store during separately authorized account setup.
- Never reads one back to you.

If anything ever asks you for a password in a chat, that is not your agent behaving correctly.
Stop and say so.

## Standing rules
A standing rule is a permission you write once so you are not asked every time. It must be
specific and it must have a limit. Anything not written here needs your explicit yes, every time.

Standing rule 1:
> ____________________________________________

Standing rule 2:
> ____________________________________________

An example of a good standing rule: "Include existing software subscriptions under 50 in the draft expense report without pausing for each line." An example of a bad one: "Use your judgment on small things."

## What you own
- Every approval.
- The threshold, and every change to it.
- The send button on every client email.
- The connection, and the right to remove it at any moment.
- Your books. Your agent analyzes copies and drafts recommendations. It never owns them.
